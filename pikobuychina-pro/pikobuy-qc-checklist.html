PikoBuy QC Photos: A Practical Inspection Checklist

QC photos are a decision point before dispatch. Read them against the exact order you placed, not against a vague expectation.

Reading time: 10 minutes · Warehouse-photo inspection guide.

Know what QC photos can show

Warehouse inspection images are useful because they show an item that has arrived for an order, rather than a polished marketplace image. PikoBuy publicly describes inspection, repacking and photo confirmation at warehouse arrival. These images can help a buyer assess visible facts before international shipping: the chosen colour, approximate shape, label or model marking, count of visible components, packaging condition and obvious damage.

Their limits are just as important. A photograph cannot reliably prove a hidden feature, long-term durability, exact composition, authenticity, safety compliance or the performance of specialised equipment. Lighting, camera angle and compression can change how a colour or surface looks. Read each image as evidence of a visible detail, not as a universal quality certificate.

Prepare an order note before the item arrives

Inspection is easiest when you have a baseline. Keep the live-listing screenshot, selected colour or model, size or measurement target, quantity, expected accessories and any specific concern in one note. The aim is not to make a huge checklist for every product; it is to make the important comparisons possible when the warehouse photos appear.

For a garment, the note may name the selected size, colour, fabric claim, chest or length measurement and print placement. For footwear, it may include size system, colourway, sole condition and included extras. For electronics, it should include exact model, plug or voltage, storage or connector type, accessories and whether the item is new or used. A focused note makes a focused photo request possible.

Check photos in a consistent order

Start with identity: does the item look like the chosen category, model and colour? Then check quantity and included accessories. Move on to visible condition: stains, tears, loose parts, cracks, dents, severe scratches, missing labels or damaged packaging. Finally check the product-specific features that matter to your decision. Compare the sequence against your saved order note, not only against memory.

If a photo is unclear, request a narrow follow-up. A useful request says what to show and how: a size label beside a ruler, a close-up of a connector, the sole edge, the internal tag, a zip in the open position, or all included accessories laid out together. “Please check everything” is difficult to verify and unlikely to resolve the question that matters.

Category checks: clothing and footwear

For clothing, inspect the size tag, visible measurements where shown, collar or neckline, seams, prints or embroidery, fasteners and the overall cut. Look for visible marks and compare the selected colour with the listing, allowing for lighting differences. If fit is crucial, measurement evidence is more useful than a letter-size label because size standards vary across sellers and markets.

For footwear, review the pair together: colour, obvious shape differences, sole condition, heel or toe construction, size label, laces and accessories. Ask for an insole or outsole measurement if it is decisive for fit. Avoid drawing conclusions from one close-up alone; a set of angles gives more reliable visible evidence.

Category checks: accessories and electronics

For bags, watches and other accessories, compare visible hardware, closures, straps, interior labels, zips and count of included pieces. Check for scratches, loose fittings or visibly damaged packaging. Do not make claims about material or long-term function that the photo cannot establish; use the image to identify what needs a more specific question.

For electronics, verify model markings, ports, plug type, included cables or accessories, visible condition and any required label. Ask for a close-up of a model number or connector when compatibility matters. A still photograph does not replace a functional test, and it should not be treated as proof of safety or regulatory suitability in the destination country.

Decide promptly and document the result

Warehouse arrival is also the moment to re-check the current return and exchange terms. PikoBuy’s public returns information describes a time-limited process for eligible items, so leave enough time to read the live conditions and act if there is a material mismatch. Save the images, your original note and any request you make. Clear records help you explain a problem accurately.

Use a simple three-way decision: approve when the visible evidence matches your criteria; ask for one targeted clarification when the decision depends on a missing view; or pause and review the live return options when there is a material mismatch. Do not ship simply because you have already waited for an item to arrive.

QC ruleCompare visible evidence with the exact variant you selected. Ask for one testable extra view when the decision depends on it.

A calm final review

Good QC review is neither blind approval nor endless suspicion. It is a practical visible check against the item definition you recorded before paying. The buyer keeps responsibility for the decision; the warehouse images give that decision better evidence.
