How to Use a LitBuy Spreadsheet: A Beginner’s Guide

A spreadsheet-style directory helps you discover products and follow links. It is a research tool, not a checkout or a guarantee that a listing is current. This independent guide explains what to check before moving from a find to an order.

1. Search by the item you actually want

Start with a product type, such as shoes, a hoodie or a jacket. A focused category is easier to compare than a long list of unrelated finds. Use a descriptive search term and open the relevant category in the database. If a link no longer opens or the item no longer matches the description, treat the record as outdated.

2. Compare the live listing

Check the selected colour, size, quantity and product specifications on the current listing. A directory price can change; the live order amount and currency conversion are the figures to verify before paying. Compare the seller’s measurements with an item you own. Note what is unknown rather than assuming that all versions are identical.

3. Keep a record of the exact option

Save the product link and your selected variant, measurement, quantity and price context. A screenshot can help you remember what you intended to choose, but it does not reserve availability or establish seller terms. Review restrictions and return conditions for the actual item before proceeding.

4. Understand the warehouse check

When warehouse photos are available for your own order, compare them with the order record. Look for the right quantity, colour and size label, visible damage and missing parts. Ask about unclear details before submitting the parcel for international shipment. Pictures support a visual check; they do not prove authenticity, comfort, durability or composition. Read the QC photo checklist for product-specific checks.

5. Budget for the whole order

The item price is only one possible cost. Depending on the order, domestic delivery, service charges, international shipping, payment conversion, taxes and customs charges may also matter. Shipping estimates depend on destination, route and packed weight or dimensions. Compare the final amount and conditions shown in your account before payment.

6. Choose the next step

Use categories to narrow your search, the product checklist to compare options, and the FAQ for order and forwarding questions. This site routes product discovery to FindSpreadsheet; it does not process purchases or provide official LitBuy support.

What a spreadsheet entry can and cannot tell you

A spreadsheet-style entry is a pointer, not an inspection certificate. Its title, image or price may have been recorded at a different time from your visit. It may link to a seller, a product category or a discovery page rather than the exact option you expect. Begin by identifying where the link actually lands and whether the listing still describes the same item. A saved row is useful for organising research, but it does not prove current stock, seller reliability or eligibility for a particular shipping route. Treat missing information as an unanswered question.

Build a shortlist before comparing prices

Choose a small number of comparable listings and write down the features that matter to you: intended use, dimensions, material description, colour and a comfortable spending range. A cheaper listing is not necessarily equivalent if it offers a different size, fewer included parts or different seller conditions. Read the description and selected option for each candidate, then compare on the same basis. If an important specification is absent, do not infer it from a similar image. Keep a note of uncertainty and remove listings that cannot answer a question essential to your decision.

Read size charts as measurements, not promises

Sizing labels are shorthand, and two sellers can use the same label for different measurements. For clothing, compare the listed garment measurements with a similar item that fits you, measuring it on a flat surface in the same way the chart describes. For footwear, check the seller’s sizing instructions and any length measurement rather than relying on a familiar regional number alone. Charts can have tolerances and measurement methods can differ. If a measurement is decisive but unclear, ask before ordering and keep the answer with the order record.

Check what is included in the selected option

The image at the top of a listing may show several colours, a set, accessories or packaging. The option selected at checkout can be narrower. Read the text attached to the exact option and compare the quantity and included parts with the price. For electronics, check the stated plug, voltage and compatibility; a photo alone cannot demonstrate that the item is safe or works with your device. If a product is restricted or the information is inconsistent, pause and check the current rules and listing details instead of trying to guess.

Use warehouse photos as a checkpoint

Once an order has reached a warehouse and photographs are available, match them to your own record rather than relying on photos from a different buyer. Count visible pieces, compare colour and labels, and inspect the angles provided. Lighting, camera perspective and packaging can make a detail difficult to judge. If a critical area is hidden, ask whether another view or measurement is available and check any current charge. A warehouse image cannot show long-term wear, comfort or authenticity. Raise questions while the item remains at the warehouse and before making the shipping decision.

Plan the parcel only after checking the item

A parcel quote depends on the destination, the selected service and the packed item details. Boxes and protective materials can increase dimensions; removing them may also increase the chance of damage. Review eligible routes and packing choices for the actual contents, especially if a product contains a battery or another restricted component. Keep product approval and shipping selection as separate decisions. If the warehouse information differs from the order record, resolve that question through the relevant order before treating the item as ready to send.

Keep a realistic cost record

Write down the amounts separately: product, domestic delivery if charged, any service or payment charge, international shipping and taxes or duties when applicable. For a hypothetical comparison, a $25 item and a $5 domestic delivery make $30 before any other charges; a shipping quote or tax has not yet been included. This example is arithmetic, not a LitBuy fee schedule or a forecast. Review the live charges and terms for your own transaction, including currency conversion and any adjustment after packing. Save the final displayed quote for later comparison.

When a link or order detail changes

If a saved link stops working, returns a different option or shows a price that no longer matches your notes, do not assume the old record still applies. Re-open the current listing and compare the item name, variant, measurements, seller information and return conditions again. If the status of an existing order is unclear, use the order’s own message or support route so that the service can identify the transaction. Avoid posting private order or tracking details in public. A new snapshot should be dated so you can distinguish it from the earlier one.

A practical first-session checklist

Before leaving the discovery page, confirm that you know where the product link leads and have recorded the exact choice. Before paying for the item, check current price, seller terms, availability and sizing. After warehouse receipt, compare your own photos with the order and raise unresolved questions. Before paying for international shipping, review destination, route eligibility, packing and total charges. Finally, retain the order confirmation and tracking information privately. These checkpoints do not guarantee an outcome, but they make it easier to notice a discrepancy while you still have a chance to ask about it.
