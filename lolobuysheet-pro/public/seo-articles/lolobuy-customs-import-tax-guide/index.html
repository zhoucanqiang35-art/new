LoloBuy Customs & Import Tax Guide (2026)

Prepare LoloBuy declarations, route-specific tax handling, customs evidence and restricted-item checks without relying on a fake safe-value formula.

By LoloBuySheet Editorial TeamReviewed October 14, 202611 min readEnglish long-form guide
Fact-checking note

This article explains LoloBuy's published process in original editorial language. Live seller terms, warehouse options, routes and prices can change; confirm them inside your account before paying.

Customs advice becomes dangerous when it is reduced to a magic declaration value. A copied number cannot account for destination law, product type, quantity, paid value, shipping route or a customs officer's assessment. LoloBuy can display route information and help prepare a parcel, but the destination authority decides whether it is inspected, taxed, released, returned or seized.

This guide uses LoloBuy's current customs, declaration, restricted-item and parcel-receipt pages checked on October 14, 2026. It is a preparation guide, not legal or tax advice. Import rules change by country and sometimes by product. Confirm the live route terms and the official customs guidance for the delivery country before paying international freight.

Separate platform handling from customs authority

LoloBuy's role is to purchase eligible goods, receive them, prepare the selected parcel and hand it to a logistics route. The platform can identify declared product information, display warnings and suggest available routes. It cannot guarantee how a foreign customs authority will classify the product or whether a parcel will be selected for inspection.

The official LoloBuy help pages explicitly mention possible rejection, taxation and confiscation. They also say the recipient bears customs-related risks. Read that before ordering. A route being available in the account is not a certificate that every product is legal to import into every destination.

Check product eligibility before warehouse storage

Customs preparation begins at the product link. Identify batteries, liquids, powders, cosmetics, food, medicine, magnetic products, plant or animal materials, weapons, cultural goods and intellectual-property risks. A product can be accepted by a seller and still be restricted by the agent, airline, logistics line or destination country.

Read LoloBuy's current prohibited and restricted items information, then check the destination authority's rules. Do not hide a restricted feature in a vague item name. If the live route list excludes the product category, changing the description does not make the physical risk disappear.

Use an accurate, itemized declaration

A useful declaration identifies the real product type, quantity and defensible value. Keep it consistent with the order record and payment evidence. “Clothing” may be too broad when the parcel contains several different products; a more specific item description can make a legitimate clearance request easier to answer.

LoloBuy's help says users may leave a note and declare value when submitting a parcel, and that some routes collect tax from the declared USD value. The platform also lists route families where tax is calculated from that declaration. Because route availability and tax handling change, use the live card rather than an old screenshot or community table.

A low declaration is not automatically a low-tax outcome. Customs can request invoices, reassess value, delay the parcel or reject inconsistent information. An artificially low value may also affect compensation if the parcel is lost or damaged. Accuracy is the safer operational rule.

There is no universal ‘safe value per kilogram’

Online communities often repeat a fixed dollar amount per kilogram. That formula ignores what the parcel contains. Two kilograms of inexpensive textiles and two kilograms of electronics do not have the same market value or regulatory profile. It also ignores country thresholds, VAT systems, duties, carrier brokerage and route-specific tax collection.

Do not use a number whose only source is “someone else's haul cleared.” One successful parcel proves only that one parcel was released under one set of facts. It does not create a rule. Check the destination's official threshold and valuation method, then declare the actual goods in a way you can support with records.

Understand route-specific tax handling

Some lines may collect tax at submission or include a tax-handling method; others may leave charges to customs or the last-mile carrier. The route card should explain accepted products, value limits, recipient requirements and whether tax is prepaid, estimated or collected later. Do not assume “tax-free” means customs-free, risk-free or exempt from every destination charge.

Compare route restrictions together with the billing method. A route that accepts the product may be expensive by volumetric weight. A cheaper line may require different recipient documents or provide weaker return options. Customs planning belongs inside the total route decision described in the LoloBuy shipping cost guide.

Prepare a clearance evidence pack before dispatch

LoloBuy's customs-inspection page says authorities may contact the recipient for an invoice or clearance documents, especially for parcels that are bulky, heavy or sensitive. A slow response can lead to return or destruction costs. Make sure the recipient name, phone, email and address are accurate and monitored.

  • LO order numbers and product descriptions
  • Seller listings and selected variants
  • Quantity and paid product value
  • Payment and balance records
  • Parcel declaration submitted to LoloBuy
  • Shipping route and tracking number
  • Warehouse and packed-weight records
  • Any requested invoice, identity or tax information
  • Messages from the carrier or customs authority

Store the evidence together before shipment. If customs contacts you, answer through the verified carrier or government channel and keep the correspondence. Do not send identity or payment documents to an unverified address copied from a random tracking message.

Parcel size, repetition and value can change attention

LoloBuy's customs-risk page notes that heavy or valuable parcels can face more inspection risk and advises against packing too many identical products together. That is a risk observation, not a guaranteed threshold. A large quantity of the same item can appear commercial even when the buyer describes it as personal.

Consolidation can reduce repeated freight charges, but an oversized, unusually valuable or repetitive parcel may not be the best customs profile. Compare a logical split where route and product rules support it. Do not split parcels to disguise value or evade legal limits; split only for genuine logistics, protection or eligibility reasons.

Know what happens when clearance fails

An undeliverable parcel may be returned, held, destroyed or confiscated depending on the reason and local law. LoloBuy's receipt notice says recipients may bear redelivery, return, customs or destruction costs when address problems, non-response or failure to cooperate causes the outcome. Some goods may have no practical return route.

Before choosing a line, read its overseas return, redelivery, disposal, insurance and compensation terms. Insurance does not normally legalize a prohibited import or guarantee compensation for inaccurate declarations. The cheapest freight quote can become the most expensive choice if the route has no recoverable return path.

Inspect the parcel and preserve arrival evidence

When the parcel arrives, inspect the outer carton, seals and apparent weight before accepting it where local rules permit. If it is visibly damaged, record that with the carrier or refuse according to the available procedure. Keep the original packaging and create a continuous unboxing record when damage or missing contents may require a claim.

This evidence is for parcel after-sales, not customs evasion. It shows what was delivered and in what condition. Apply through the live parcel record immediately because LoloBuy's current after-sales page contains overlapping time limits and the shortest warning is the safest operational deadline.

A compliant customs workflow

  1. Check whether the product is legal and route-eligible before purchase.
  2. Keep the original order and value evidence.
  3. Finish QC and identify every parcel item accurately.
  4. Read the live route's tax and recipient requirements.
  5. Submit a truthful itemized declaration.
  6. Save the declaration, packed weight and route terms.
  7. Monitor carrier and customs messages.
  8. Respond promptly through verified channels.
  9. Preserve arrival and unboxing evidence.

Good customs planning does not promise that a parcel will avoid inspection or tax. It makes the parcel explainable. Accurate descriptions, defensible values, compatible goods, a monitored recipient and organized evidence reduce preventable delays and make legitimate questions easier to answer.
