How to Use a LitBuy Spreadsheet: A Beginner’s Guide to Finds, QC and Ordering

LitBuy · QC · Shipping

A LitBuy spreadsheet is a research directory: it brings product links and notes together so you can compare potential finds. It is not the seller, a guarantee of stock, or proof that an item will look exactly like a photograph. The decision to purchase belongs on the current listing, and the decision to ship comes later, after the order reaches the warehouse and has been inspected.

This guide follows one item from discovery to a parcel decision. It explains what a useful row can tell you, how to check the live listing, what to record when ordering, and how warehouse QC photos differ from seller images. Prices, availability, service terms and eligible routes can change; verify them before paying.

1. Start with a specific product, not an enormous list

A large directory is most useful when you already know what you want. Search by product type and a meaningful feature: “black zip hoodie,” “canvas shoulder bag,” or “running shoes.” Use the directory’s categories to narrow results, and compare a few relevant options instead of opening every listing. A row may include a product name, an image, a price, a source link or a QC reference. The amount of information varies between directories and listings.

Treat the row as a starting point. A price can be old, a link can stop working, and a seller may change the options on the same page. A find with a low displayed price is not necessarily the least expensive purchase once domestic shipping, optional services and international transport are included. Keep a short list of candidates and compare their live details before choosing.

2. Check the live link and selected variant

Open the current product page and confirm that it still describes the item you found. Check the seller’s options, color, model, size, quantity and any variation that changes what is shipped. If a listing combines several versions, read the option labels carefully instead of relying on the first image. Save the source link and note the selected variant so you can compare it again when the item arrives.

For clothing, compare the seller’s measurements with a similar item you already own. Chest width, garment length and sleeve length can be more useful than a familiar letter size. For shoes, check the seller’s sizing guidance and consider an insole measurement if available. A size chart can itself be approximate, so ask for clarification when the description, photographs and measurements do not agree.

A spreadsheet may also show historical buyer photos or notes. These can help you decide what to inspect, but they might depict a different size, seller option or earlier batch. They are not evidence of the exact item in your own order. If the listing is unavailable or the option is unclear, pause rather than trying to solve the uncertainty after payment.

3. Understand the separate costs before ordering

The displayed item price is only one part of the budget. Depending on the specific order and current terms, costs can include domestic delivery to the warehouse, service or optional inspection charges, international shipping, and taxes, duties or carrier charges at the destination. Check which amounts are actually shown for your order; do not assume every possible charge applies or that an advertised estimate is the final amount.

LitBuy’s freight estimator can help you plan a range using the information available before packing. The final quote depends on the destination, actual packed parcel, available line and selected services. A bulky light item may be charged differently from a compact item of the same actual weight if a line uses volumetric weight. Leave a budget buffer, especially when combining shoes, boxes or protective packaging.

4. Submit the order and keep a record

Once you have chosen a live listing, enter the exact option and quantity in the purchasing workflow and check the order summary before payment. Keep the link, screenshots or notes describing the option, the size chart and the order ID together. Those details make it easier to compare the received item and to ask a precise question if something does not match.

The seller must still accept and send the order. A directory cannot guarantee stock or seller dispatch. Monitor the order status in your account and follow the current support process if the purchase is unavailable, delayed or differs from the submitted instructions. Avoid adding restricted items to a parcel without checking both the route rules and the destination’s laws.

5. Compare your own warehouse QC photos

When the item reaches the warehouse, compare the QC evidence with the order you actually placed. Start with the whole item: is the model, color and quantity right? Then inspect details that matter for that category. For shoes, look at overall shape, symmetry, soles and any requested measurement. For a bag, check shape, handles, closures and visible stitching. For clothing, look at labels, front and back, and measurements relevant to fit.

Seller photos advertise a product; another buyer’s QC photos describe a different received item; your warehouse photos document what was photographed for your order. None can prove authenticity, long-term durability, hidden defects or how the product will fit you. If a necessary angle or measurement is missing, check the current availability and price of extra inspection before requesting it.

If the item materially differs from the order, act while it is in the warehouse. Save the relevant photos, identify the discrepancy, and consult the current domestic-return and after-sales rules for that seller and order. Eligibility, deadlines and outcomes can vary. A clear request such as “the received color differs from the selected option” is easier to assess than a general complaint.

6. Decide what belongs in one parcel

After inspection, review the items together. Combining orders can reduce the number of separate parcels, but it does not always reduce the total cost. Heavy shoes beside delicate accessories may need extra protection; large boxes can increase dimensions; an item subject to restrictions can limit route options. Compare the convenience and quote with the risk and packaging requirements of the actual contents.

Check each item’s live warehouse storage deadline rather than assuming all orders expire together. Before submitting a parcel, choose appropriate protection and compare the routes currently available for the destination. Read the rules for prohibited items, insurance and compensation separately, and declare the contents and value accurately according to applicable requirements. An estimate or tracking update is not a delivery guarantee.

A simple first-order checklist

  • 01Search for a specific product and shortlist relevant finds.
  • 02Open the current listing and confirm the exact variant and measurements.
  • 03Budget for the item and potential delivery, service, international shipping and import costs.
  • 04Save the source link, option, order ID and measurement information.
  • 05Compare your own warehouse photos with the order before deciding to ship.
  • 06Check storage deadlines, route eligibility, packaging and the final parcel quote.
  • 07Retain the tracking number and current service terms until the order is complete.
