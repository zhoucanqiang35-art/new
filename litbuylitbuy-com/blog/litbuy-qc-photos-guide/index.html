LitBuy QC Photos: What to Check Before Shipping Your Parcel

LitBuy · QC · Shipping

Warehouse QC photos give you a chance to compare the item that arrived with the option you ordered before it is sent internationally. Start with the whole item, then check the details that matter for its category. A clear mismatch is easier to address while the order remains in the domestic warehouse than after a parcel has been shipped.

A photograph has limits: it cannot establish authenticity, guarantee durability, reveal every hidden defect or prove how an item will fit. This guide helps you use the available evidence carefully, ask for a missing view when necessary and make a timely decision under the current return and support rules.

1. Identify which photos you are viewing

A seller’s listing photos are promotional images of an offered product. A spreadsheet may also link to another buyer’s photos, which can provide useful examples but may show a different order, option, size or production period. Your own warehouse QC photos document what was photographed for your order. Keep those sources separate when comparing evidence.

LitBuy publicly advertises inspection and QC photos when items reach its warehouse. The actual views and any available additional inspection depend on the current service and order. Do not assume a specific number of photos, free extras or a turnaround time without checking the live terms. If a crucial feature is not visible, check whether an additional view or measurement is available and what it costs.

2. Keep the original order details nearby

Before judging fine details, retrieve the live listing or the record you saved at purchase. Compare the product, model, selected color or version, size and quantity with the order confirmation. A single listing may contain several choices that look similar in a thumbnail. Check whether the received option is the one actually selected, not just the most prominent image on the seller’s page.

Save the product link, order ID, seller description and relevant size chart. If the listing has changed, screenshots or order notes can help explain what you selected at the time. Compare evidence fairly: lighting, perspective, display settings and compression may change how a color or surface appears. Ask for clarification when the difference is important and the existing images are inconclusive.

3. Inspect the whole item before close-ups

Start with a front, back or overall view where available. Is the expected item present, and is the overall shape consistent with the ordered model? For paired items such as shoes, compare left and right rather than examining one attractive close-up. For garments, look at the entire front and back. For bags, review the body, handles and closure in context.

Then look for obvious damage or inconsistencies: tears, missing parts, severe stains, broken fasteners or visible deformation. An image may not show whether a zipper works or whether an internal component is damaged. Describe only what you can actually see, and request a useful angle if the existing view cannot answer an important question.

4. Check measurements when fit matters

For clothing, compare relevant garment measurements with the seller’s chart and a similar item you already own. Chest width, length, shoulder width and sleeve length can be more informative than a letter size. For footwear, an insole measurement may help when it is available and measured consistently. For a bag, dimensions can indicate whether it fits the intended use.

Photographs of a tape measure require context. Check where the measurement begins and ends, whether the item is laid flat and whether the scale can be read. Different measuring methods and tolerances can produce small differences. A single image cannot guarantee personal fit; use the measurement as one piece of evidence alongside the seller’s description.

5. Tailor the checklist to the product

For shoes, compare overall shape, the pair’s apparent symmetry, soles, labels, the requested size and visible condition. The box may matter if it is part of the intended purchase or packaging plan. A photo cannot reveal all comfort or durability issues. If the angle hides the feature that determines your decision, ask whether a clearer photo or measurement is possible.

For clothing, check the color and size label, front and back alignment, visible seams, fasteners and category-specific measurements. Fabric appearance in a photograph may not establish composition or thickness. If a material detail is important, compare the seller’s description and label and seek clarification instead of treating a screen image as a laboratory test.

For bags and accessories, inspect the overall shape, handles or straps, attachment points, closures, lining where visible and any included components. For electronics, compare the exact model, connector or plug, visible condition and included accessories, then separately check the shipping route’s rules. A warehouse image does not necessarily demonstrate electrical performance or safety.

6. Decide whether to keep, ask or seek a return

If the item matches your expectations and the evidence is sufficient, keep the inspection record and proceed to the parcel decision. If the evidence is incomplete, make a narrow request: identify the feature, the angle or the measurement you need, and check the current charge and availability before confirming the service.

If there is a material mismatch, save the relevant images and compare them with the original order. Contact support with the order ID and a concise description, such as the selected option versus the visible received option. Review the applicable domestic-return and after-sales rules promptly. Seller policies, item categories, deadlines and outcomes can differ; a request does not guarantee acceptance or reimbursement.

Avoid turning a minor photographic difference into a claim of a defect without enough evidence. Equally, do not approve shipment when an important discrepancy remains unresolved. Decide based on the information available, the time remaining in storage and the actual policies governing the order.

7. Save the evidence through delivery

Keep the order ID, source link, QC images, support messages and any return decision together. Once the parcel is prepared, add the packing details, selected line, tracking number and applicable terms. If a problem arises later, a clear record helps distinguish a warehouse-stage question from damage or loss during international transport.

Shipping insurance and QC are separate matters. Inspection photos do not create automatic coverage, and a route’s insurance or compensation conditions may contain exclusions and deadlines. Read the current terms before shipping and follow the stated process if you need help.

Quick QC checklist

  • 01Confirm the item, variant, size, color and quantity against the order.
  • 02Inspect the overall item or pair before focusing on details.
  • 03Compare relevant measurements using a consistent method.
  • 04Check visible labels, fasteners, attachments and condition for the category.
  • 05Identify anything important that the photos do not show.
  • 06Request clarification or review the current return rules before the deadline.
  • 07Save the evidence and decide whether the item is suitable to ship.
