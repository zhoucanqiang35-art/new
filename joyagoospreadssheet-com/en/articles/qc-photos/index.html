How to use Joyagoo QC photos before you commit to a parcel

What Joyagoo QC photos are for

Joyagoo describes a quality check after an item reaches its warehouse, with product photographs available in the order area. These images help you compare the received item with your own order before you decide whether to keep it, ask a question or seek a return. A seller’s catalogue picture is an advertisement; a warehouse photograph is evidence of the item shown in that particular image. Neither type of picture is a guarantee of quality, authenticity or future delivery condition. Open the order record beside the listing and write down the seller, colour, size, quantity and any special option. Compare those selections with the overall warehouse views. Similar colours and versions may look alike, so check labels and distinguishing details instead of relying on the product title alone. If the listing has changed since purchase, your saved order confirmation and screenshots may be more useful than the current product page.

Check the complete item first

Look for the expected number of pieces, major damage, stains, obvious construction problems and the correct visible variant. Zoom in, but remember that shadows, lighting and compression can change the apparent colour or texture. A detail outside the camera frame remains unknown. If the available pictures do not show a decision-critical area, ask whether the warehouse can provide an additional view and confirm any charge before ordering it. Compare the left and right shoes, toe shape, heels, soles, labels, visible glue or damage and the colour of included laces. Check the seller’s sizing chart and ask for an insole measurement when that measurement would change your choice. A label alone does not prove fit: compare a measurement with footwear that fits, note how it was measured and allow for differences between methods. Decide separately whether the original box is worth its shipping volume and protection.

Clothing: compare measurements and details

Check the front, back, collar, seams, cuffs and any prints or embroidery. Compare the chest, length, sleeve or other relevant measurements with a garment measured in the same way, rather than treating S, M or L as universal sizes. Confirm both sides of a graphic and any selected personalisation. A photograph can reveal a visible defect, but it cannot establish how fabric will wear or whether every composition claim is accurate.

Accessories, sets and electronics

For a bag or accessory, check visible surfaces, handles, closures and included pieces. For a set, count the photographed components and compare them with the order. For electronics, verify the visible model, plug, ports, cables and exterior condition, then check compatibility and shipping restrictions. Joyagoo states that its electronics inspection is visual and does not include powering an item on to test quality. Do not interpret a photo as a functional or safety test. Make a specific request: identify the order, the part to photograph, the measurement and the reason it matters. For example, ask for a clear view of the back print or a measurement from one seam to another. Confirm whether extra photos, measuring or another service is available and what it costs. Preserve the reply with your order. A precise request makes the result easier to compare and avoids assuming that a particular inspection is included.

Make the decision before the return deadline

Joyagoo’s published shopping guide describes a return or exchange request within five days after QC completion. That guidance does not mean that every product is eligible or that a request automatically succeeds. Check the current order, seller conditions, item state, timing and potential costs. If you see a discrepancy, contact the agent promptly through the order and describe the exact difference and desired resolution. Do not submit the item in an international parcel while a return decision remains unresolved. Save the order identifier, listing, selected option, price, warehouse status, QC images, date and any response. For a measurement dispute, retain the seller’s chart and the photo showing the measuring points. For a visible defect, identify the relevant image and area. A dated record helps support investigate and keeps questions about a single item separate from a later question about a combined parcel.

After approval, plan the parcel

Once each item is resolved, review the destination, product restrictions, packaging, available route and final shipping quote. Product payment and international shipping are separate stages, and taxes or other charges may also apply. Keep the parcel number and tracking events after dispatch. The available QC evidence supports an informed decision; it cannot guarantee customs clearance, a delivery date or the condition after transit.

A label matches, but the measurements do not

Place the seller’s chart beside the measurement photo and confirm the units and the points used. A chest circumference is not the same as a garment laid flat across the chest. Ask for a clear repeat measurement if the method is uncertain; compare the result with a garment measured in the same way.

A mark is visible in one photograph

Check whether another view shows the same area and whether the lighting could be misleading. Request a close view if it would change your decision. Record the order number and point to the relevant image; ask whether the seller accepts a return before assuming a refund.

An accessory is hidden from view

A closed box or an out-of-frame compartment cannot confirm what is inside. Compare the order’s contents with the visible parts and ask for a specific view where available. Confirm any extra charge first and do not treat the absence of a photograph as proof that a part is missing.

The parcel is almost ready

Review each item separately before grouping it with the others. If one item still needs a return decision, note its deadline and ask whether the remaining items can be submitted separately. Compare protection and route restrictions before paying; QC photos do not guarantee transit condition or customs clearance.
