HipoBuy QC Photos: A Practical Inspection Checklist

Independent product research

Start with the details

QC photos provide visual evidence to compare with the item and variant you selected. They do not establish authenticity, fit, electrical safety or long-term durability. Start by matching the record, colour, size and quantity to your order before examining details.

For shoes, compare both sides, the pair’s shape, soles, labels and any visible damage. For clothing, check seams, print placement, colour and size labels. For bags, look at handles, closures, straps and hardware. Lighting and camera angles can change the apparent colour or shape, so avoid making a fine distinction from one image.

When size matters, request a clear measurement with the tape positioned on the relevant part of the item. Compare it with the seller’s measurement method and a similar item that fits you. Allow for normal measurement differences and clarify whether the chart uses flat dimensions or body circumference.

Begin with identification. Match the order reference, quantity, colour and size label to your notes. If a listing has more than one option, check that the image is associated with your chosen option rather than a generic seller picture. Keep the product listing and the available inspection images together so you can make a like-for-like comparison.

For garments, photograph or request views of the front and back, seams, print, fastenings and the size label where these details matter. A close view can help distinguish a loose thread from a mark in the image, but lighting and compression still limit what a photograph can prove. Note precisely which detail is unclear instead of relying on a general impression.

Compare the evidence

For a pair of shoes, compare left and right as a pair: overall shape, panels, stitching, sole and labels. If the intended size is important, a photo showing an insole measurement may be more useful than a label alone. Check how the tape is positioned and compare the same measurement points with a shoe that already fits.

For accessories or electronics, verify visible components and included pieces against the current listing. A photograph cannot verify battery condition, electrical compatibility, water resistance or future performance. If the decision depends on a functional feature, seek reliable confirmation rather than interpreting an image as a test result.

Write a brief decision note: the issue, which image shows it, and what additional evidence you need. Check the current policy and timing before contacting the relevant service or seller. If a return or exchange is available, ask about eligibility and charges; do not assume the same terms apply to every item or order.

Start by identifying the evidence you are looking at. A seller’s promotional photograph, a historical image and an image associated with your own order can answer different questions. Check the order reference and the selected size, colour and quantity before comparing fine details. If the source or variant of an image is unclear, record that limitation. A close match in appearance does not prove that two photographs show the same physical item.

Prepare a short checklist before opening the images. List the details that would change your decision, such as the size label, a seam, a closure, the number of pieces or a visible measurement. Review the broad views first to establish which item is shown, then inspect close views. This sequence reduces the chance of focusing on a minor mark while missing a wrong colour or incomplete set.

Consider lighting, perspective and image quality. Different camera angles can make a shape look wider or narrower; bright or warm lighting can affect apparent colour. A compressed image may obscure a small mark. Where a detail matters, request another angle or a closer view if the service offers that option. Describe the precise area rather than asking only for a generic “better photo.”

Make the decision

For garments, compare front and back, seams, stitching, print placement, fastenings and size labels with the selected option and listing. A photograph may show a visible defect, but it does not test durability after washing or wearing. If a measurement is important, ask for the measurement point and tape placement to be visible. Compare the same points with the seller chart and a reference item that fits.

For footwear, review both shoes together and look at shape, panels, soles, stitching and size labels. A pair can look consistent from one side while a detail is hidden on the other. An insole measurement can help with length, but it cannot establish comfort, width or fit by itself. If packaging is important, check the current listing and available image rather than assuming a box will be included.

For bags, watches or small accessories, check the shape, fastenings, straps, visible hardware and included pieces against the selected listing. For electronics, verify visible model markings and accessories only where legible. A still image is not a functional or electrical safety test. If compatibility, battery condition or performance is essential, seek appropriate confirmation through the relevant service.

Separate a definite mismatch from an unanswered question. A visible wrong size label is different from a colour that is hard to judge under unusual lighting. Save the relevant image and make a specific note of the difference, the expected option and the order reference. Ask for clarification or use the support process while the applicable review window remains open; check that window in the current terms.

Before approving a parcel, revisit unresolved items and the current return or exchange conditions. Eligibility, timing and costs may depend on the seller, product and service. Do not promise a refund or assume that an inspection photo guarantees authenticity, fit or future quality. The purpose of QC review is to make a more informed decision from the evidence available, while keeping the limitations of that evidence visible.

If a critical area is hidden, ask for a closer image where the service offers that option. Record the discrepancy, the image and the order reference before contacting support. Review current return conditions and any deadlines before choosing whether to approve, request more information or seek a remedy.
