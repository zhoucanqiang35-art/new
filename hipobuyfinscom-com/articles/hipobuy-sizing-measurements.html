HipoBuy Sizing: Compare Shoe and Clothing Measurements

Independent product research

Size labels are not a universal measurement. Start with the seller’s current chart and compare its units and measuring method with an item that already fits. Record the intended fit as well: a close fit and a loose layer may require different comparisons.

For a top, measure a similar garment flat across the chest, shoulders, length and sleeves where relevant. Check whether a chart lists a flat width or a full circumference before comparing numbers. Measure trousers at the waist, rise, inseam and leg opening if those dimensions affect the fit.

For shoes, compare the seller’s chart with the length of a suitable insole where possible. Sizes and widths vary by design. A length alone does not describe toe shape, arch support or comfort, so treat it as one piece of evidence rather than a guarantee.

If QC measurements are offered, ask for clear placement of the tape and compare the same points and units. Leave room for ordinary measurement variation, and review the seller’s current exchange or return conditions before purchasing. Photos and charts reduce uncertainty but cannot guarantee a fit.
