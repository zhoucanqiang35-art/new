How to Use a HipoBuy Spreadsheet to Compare Product Links

Independent product research

Start with the details

A HipoBuy spreadsheet is a way to organise potential product finds before making a decision. Begin with a category, a model or a feature you can describe. Open the live listing and verify its current seller, options, images and displayed price; a stored spreadsheet entry may have changed since it was added.

Build a short comparison record for each candidate: listing link, seller, exact colour or size, source price, relevant photos and the date checked. Similar thumbnails do not prove that two records contain the same product. Check the selected option against the seller’s images and description before treating two prices as directly comparable.

Use category pages to narrow a broad search and product pages to inspect one candidate. If a link no longer opens, look for a current listing rather than assuming a replacement is identical. Check any available QC evidence against the selected variant and note questions that photos do not answer.

Define what you need before searching. A clear description might include the intended use, material, colour, dimensions and the features you must be able to check. For clothing, note the fit and measurements; for a bag, note its capacity and strap length. These requirements make it easier to reject a result that looks attractive but lacks the information needed for your decision. They also make a spreadsheet more useful than a list of unrelated links.

Compare the evidence

Create one row per distinct listing and variant. Useful columns include the link, seller name, date checked, selected option, displayed currency, source price, size information, image notes and status. Keep estimates separate from amounts already shown in the live checkout. A note saying “price unknown for selected size” is more useful than copying a price that belongs to a different option. If you revisit a row later, check the seller and option again.

Compare like with like. Two listings can show the same product photograph but offer different materials, quantities or versions. Open each record and read the available option labels and description. When the product has accessories, packaging or multiple pieces, record exactly what the selected option appears to include. If the listing does not answer a question important to you, mark that field as unresolved rather than filling it from a similar listing.

Use the size chart as part of the record rather than treating the size letter as a universal standard. Note whether the chart describes an item measured flat or a body measurement, the units used and the points being measured. Compare it with a similar garment or shoe you already know. If the relevant measurement is absent, the spreadsheet can remind you to seek clarification before making a commitment.

Images have different roles. A listing image can show the seller’s presentation; an available QC image may show a specific item or variant. Neither automatically establishes that every future item from the listing will be identical. Compare visible colour, construction and labels with your chosen option, and keep the source of each image clear. A photograph cannot establish long-term performance, electrical safety or authenticity.

Make the decision

Review the cost in stages. The listing price may be followed by domestic delivery, optional services, international shipping and possible destination charges. Record each line separately as the information becomes available. Do not insert an estimated parcel cost into the product-price column or describe an old source price as a guaranteed delivered price. Recheck currency and the actual amount at each current confirmation screen.

A saved link can stop working or lead to a changed listing. If that happens, retain a note explaining what changed and search for an alternative using the features you recorded. Do not silently replace the link and assume it refers to the same seller or product. Recheck the new listing’s variant, measurements, images and current terms before comparing it with earlier candidates.

Before ordering, reduce the list to the candidates you understand and identify any remaining uncertainty. Save the exact option and the live order reference. After evidence becomes available, compare it with the record and decide whether to proceed, request more information where offered, or use the relevant support process. The spreadsheet supports those decisions; it does not reserve stock, process payment or promise a particular outcome.

An item price is only one part of the eventual cost. Record domestic delivery, service charges if any, international shipping and possible destination charges separately as they become available. Recheck the live selection and total before payment. This independent site links to FindSpreadsheet for product discovery and does not process orders.
