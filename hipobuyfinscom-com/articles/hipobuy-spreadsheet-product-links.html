How to Use a HipoBuy Spreadsheet to Compare Product Links

Independent product research

Start with the details

A HipoBuy spreadsheet is a way to organise potential product finds before making a decision. Begin with a category, a model or a feature you can describe. Open the live listing and verify its current seller, options, images and displayed price; a stored spreadsheet entry may have changed since it was added.

Build a short comparison record for each candidate: listing link, seller, exact colour or size, source price, relevant photos and the date checked. Similar thumbnails do not prove that two records contain the same product. Check the selected option against the seller’s images and description before treating two prices as directly comparable.

Use category pages to narrow a broad search and product pages to inspect one candidate. If a link no longer opens, look for a current listing rather than assuming a replacement is identical. Check any available QC evidence against the selected variant and note questions that photos do not answer.

Compare the evidence

Make the first comparison about a decision, not the number of links. For example, if you need a lightweight jacket, note the material, chest measurement, lining and colour shown on each listing. A result labelled simply “jacket” may group a thin shell and a lined version under the same title. Select the intended option before recording its price.

Treat the seller’s chart and listing photos as separate evidence. A chart can describe dimensions without showing how a garment looks; an image can show shape without proving measurements. If either is missing, write down the uncertainty. A lower price may not be the best choice if you cannot identify the variant or the information needed for sizing.

Use a simple status column: candidate, checked, needs clarification or unavailable. Add the date because stock, price and pictures can change. When you return later, reopen the live record and compare the selected option again. Do not assume a link saved by another shopper points to the same configuration you intend to buy.

Make the decision

If a listing offers several sizes or colours, record the exact option text as well as the link. Where photos are available for the selected item, compare its visible features against that record. A product card can be useful for discovery, but it cannot show every condition that applies at checkout.

Before moving on to parcel planning, remove candidates you no longer intend to order and keep a separate note for unresolved questions. This prevents an old price or abandoned variation from becoming the reference for a later decision. The current listing and checkout remain the place to confirm availability and the amount you will actually pay.

An item price is only one part of the eventual cost. Record domestic delivery, service charges if any, international shipping and possible destination charges separately as they become available. Recheck the live selection and total before payment. This independent site links to FindSpreadsheet for product discovery and does not process orders.
