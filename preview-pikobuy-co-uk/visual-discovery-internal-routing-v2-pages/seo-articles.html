ARTICLE 01 · PIKOBUY GUIDE

How to Use PikoBuy for Taobao, Weidian and 1688: a source-first workflow

Approx. 1,350 words · Updated October 2026 · Read official rules before paying

A good PikoBuy order starts before the checkout screen. The hard part is not pasting a link; it is deciding whether the source listing, variant and total cost make sense. That is why the most useful workflow is source-first: read the original listing, capture the exact option you want, then use PikoBuy to move through ordering, warehouse inspection and parcel submission in separate decisions.

PikoBuy’s own shopping guide describes a six-step flow: select an item, paste a link or search, submit and pay for the purchasing order, wait for warehouse inspection, submit an international parcel and then follow tracking. That sequence is important because a product payment is not the same decision as paying for international shipping. Treat each stage as a checkpoint instead of assuming that one displayed price covers the final delivered cost.

1. Start with the original product information

Save the product URL, title, seller images, option names, size chart and stated domestic delivery terms. For Taobao, Weidian and 1688 listings, the variation labels can be terse or image-based. The label “black” may not identify the same material, season or version as another black option. If a listing has a measurement chart, compare the garment measurements with an item that fits you rather than relying only on an international size label.

Use the source title and item identifier as your anchor. A spreadsheet card is a discovery tool, not proof of stock, authenticity, seller reputation or final price. PikoBuy’s service agreement says it provides product search/display and purchasing or forwarding services and is not the actual seller or sales platform. In practice, that means the seller’s listing remains the place to check the variation, stock signals and seller-specific conditions.

2. Submit a precise purchasing order

Paste the source link into PikoBuy, choose the option deliberately and review the first payment before confirming. The official guide notes that the price is subject to the actual purchase and that an out-of-stock order is refunded. That is a reason to avoid promising a fixed price to yourself before the order is actually processed. Exchange-rate movement, the selected variant and domestic shipping can change the amount shown at different stages.

Write a concise note only when it resolves a real ambiguity: for example, “please purchase the blue option shown in the seller’s second image.” Do not use a note as a substitute for understanding the listing. If the option list is unclear, ask customer support or use the original seller information before paying rather than assuming an agent can infer your preference.

3. Use warehouse arrival as a verification moment

When an item reaches the warehouse, PikoBuy says it performs check-in, photo confirmation and a defect check. Its shipping terms also say forwarded goods are unpacked and inspected, while certain special or professional products cannot receive professional inspection. This is useful evidence, but it is not a guarantee of authenticity, internal functionality or long-term durability. Product packaging may not be opened for many categories, and an exterior photo cannot answer every question.

Look for simple, decision-relevant comparisons: color against the seller photo, logo placement, obvious stains or tears, count of pieces, visible size tag, left/right pair consistency and overall shape. If the concern is specific, use an additional detailed photo rather than trying to infer a close-up from a wide warehouse image. Keep your request factual: ask for a label, measurement point, stitching area or accessory count.

4. Separate item value from parcel strategy

Do not submit a parcel the moment one item arrives if you are still waiting for related items or need to make a return decision. PikoBuy’s shipping estimator asks for destination, weight and dimensions and compares available routes. That reinforces a practical point: route availability, billing method and delivery time can differ. Estimate the parcel after you know what you are actually keeping, and leave room for packaging, volumetric weight and destination-specific taxes or duties.

Choose a route based on the real trade-off you can accept, not a headline delivery time. PikoBuy’s terms say cross-border transactions can involve duties, taxes, brokerage fees or other charges, and its shipping terms say third-party logistics providers carry the parcels. International transport has customs and carrier risk. The responsible approach is to budget for uncertainty and to read the route information shown at submission.

5. Keep a clear record until delivery

Save the item link, selected variation, warehouse photos, parcel submission information and tracking number in one place. When tracking appears, PikoBuy’s guide says it is generally available within three days after shipment; international delivery can take longer. When receiving a parcel, inspect the exterior packaging before confirming receipt. If there is an issue, use the platform’s available support route promptly and preserve photographs of the parcel condition.

Bottom line: PikoBuy can simplify the order-to-warehouse-to-parcel flow, but it does not remove the need to validate the seller listing, selected option, warehouse evidence and shipping terms yourself.
  • Save the original listing and exact variation before paying.
  • Use warehouse photos to answer concrete visible questions.
  • Check return eligibility before parcel submission.
  • Compare shipping routes using actual destination, weight and dimensions.
  • Budget separately for product cost, domestic charges, international shipping and possible taxes.
ARTICLE 02 · QC, RETURNS & SHIPPING

How to read PikoBuy QC photos, returns and shipping decisions without guessing

Approx. 1,320 words · Updated October 2026 · Evidence first, assumptions last

Warehouse photos are valuable because they give you a chance to decide before international shipping. They are not a verdict on quality. A useful QC review is a narrow comparison between what the seller represented, what you selected and what is visibly in the warehouse. The moment you treat a photograph as proof of authenticity or perfect functionality, you are asking it to answer a question it cannot reliably answer.

PikoBuy’s guide says warehouse inspection includes check-in, photo confirmation and a defect check. Its terms also limit what can be professionally inspected for certain items, particularly where packaging cannot be opened or specialist evaluation is needed. The practical response is not to abandon QC photos; it is to use them for the questions photos can answer and to avoid overstating the result.

Build a QC checklist around the item you actually ordered

Open the original listing next to the warehouse images. First verify identity: seller title, colorway, quantity and any easy-to-read size or model tag. Then examine visible condition: marks, stains, scratches, loose threads, dents, warped shape, pair symmetry or missing accessories. A shoe photo can show whether the two shoes appear to match in color and construction; it cannot establish future comfort or authenticate every material. A jacket photo can show a label, zipper alignment or print placement; it cannot substitute for a full fit test.

Use the listing’s own details as the comparison standard. Images from reviews, social media or resale listings may show a different production batch, seller or version. If you do request extra photos, ask for an observable detail: “close-up of size tag,” “front and back of both shoes,” “measurement from pit to pit,” or “photo of included cable.” Vague requests such as “check if it is good” create ambiguity for everyone.

Know when a warehouse photo should trigger a return question

If photos show the wrong color, wrong model, visible damage or a missing component, document the specific mismatch with the relevant listing screenshot. PikoBuy’s return page says an item may be returnable when the seller offers the relevant guarantee, it remains in resalable condition, and it has been in the warehouse no more than five days. It also distinguishes between a user’s change-of-mind return and a seller-caused problem such as the wrong item or a quality issue.

That timing matters. The official return page measures the application window from the status changing to “In Warehouse,” and says late requests may not be accepted. It lists exclusions such as customised products, some intimate apparel, second-hand goods, food and other seller-specified items. Do not assume a return is free or automatic. Where an item has no quality issue, the page lists return shipping, the seller’s original shipping and an RMB 5 service fee for an unconditional return; the seller’s actual acceptance and rules still matter.

Do the shipping maths after your keep/return decision

Once you choose which items to keep, evaluate the parcel. The official shipping estimate asks for destination, estimated weight and dimensions, and compares routes. This is why an individual item’s price is incomplete as a purchasing decision. Volumetric weight, packaging, route restrictions and destination taxes can change the final cost. PikoBuy’s terms also state that duties, taxes, brokerage fees and other cross-border charges may apply and are the user’s responsibility.

Read route names, billing method and the items you are including. The official guide advises users to choose according to their needs because routes differ in delivery time and billing method. A slower route may suit a low-value, non-urgent parcel; an option with a different billing method may not be comparable on a simple “cheapest” label. Avoid combining products that a route does not accept, and do not rely on a general internet claim about prohibited items. Check the current destination and route rules shown during submission.

Use cautious language around risk

PikoBuy’s shipping terms explain that parcels are carried by third-party logistics providers and that customs policies and other uncontrollable factors can create risk. That does not mean every parcel has a problem; it means no honest guide should promise clearance, an arrival date or compensation outside the published terms. If insurance is offered for a particular parcel, read the current policy and exclusions before buying it. Keep the invoice, warehouse photos and tracking record because those are the practical materials you will need if a carrier or support discussion becomes necessary.

A sensible rule: decide on an item while it is still in the warehouse; decide on a shipping route only after you know the parcel you are actually sending.
  • Compare QC photos against the chosen seller option, not a random reference image.
  • Ask for one precise extra photo when a visible fact is missing.
  • Review the return window as soon as the order enters the warehouse.
  • Use the shipping estimator with your real destination and expected parcel measurements.
  • Never treat QC or shipping estimates as guarantees of authenticity, delivery or customs clearance.
