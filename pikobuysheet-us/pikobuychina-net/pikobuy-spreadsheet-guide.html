PikoBuy Spreadsheet Guide: Build a Smarter China Shopping List

A spreadsheet cannot guarantee quality or delivery. Used well, however, it can stop product links, option details and warehouse evidence from becoming a trail of forgotten tabs.

Independent research · reviewed 9 September 2026

Shopping from Chinese marketplaces often begins messily. You open several product listings, compare prices in different currencies, save screenshots, and try to remember which colour or size belonged to which seller. A simple spreadsheet turns that browsing session into a controlled shortlist. It does not make a seller reliable, authenticate a product, reserve stock or predict international shipping. It records what you know, what you still need to verify and what changed before payment.

This distinction matters because PikoBuy’s public pages do not describe a spreadsheet as an official platform feature. PikoBuy says buyers can search directly or submit product links, images or sourcing requests, and its beginner guide tells users to confirm specifications, colour and size on source platforms before ordering. In this guide, “PikoBuy spreadsheet” means a buyer-created planning document that supports that official workflow. It is a research tool, not a substitute for the live listing, warehouse photos or the options shown in your PikoBuy account.

Start with the decision, not with dozens of links

Before collecting products, write one sentence describing what you are trying to buy. Include the product type, non-negotiable specification and maximum uncertainty you will accept. “Black cotton hoodie, seller chart available, exact chest measurement required” is more useful than “hoodies.” This forces comparable candidates into the same group and makes obvious mismatches easier to delete.

Keep separate tabs for substantially different product types. Shoes, electronics and decorative objects need different evidence, so a universal score quickly becomes misleading. PikoBuy’s beginner guide names Taobao, Tmall, 1688, Weidian and Yupoo among the places where buyers may find items. Record the actual source platform and store information instead of treating every link as interchangeable. The guide itself recommends saving the item or store information for easier ordering.

Use columns that preserve the exact offer

Your first group of columns should identify the candidate: a short item name, source URL, seller or store name, date checked and a saved screenshot reference. The date matters because listings can change. A spreadsheet row copied last week is evidence of what you saw then, not proof of today’s stock, options or price. Open the source again immediately before submitting an order.

The second group should capture the offer you actually want: colour, size, model, version, quantity, included pieces and any seller measurement that affects the decision. Do not write “same as photo.” Product pages frequently show several variants, and the hero image may not represent the selected option. PikoBuy’s official ordering sequence explicitly asks the buyer to choose specifications such as colour, size and quantity before submitting the purchasing order and making the first payment.

Separate observed facts from your judgement

Add two columns called “confirmed evidence” and “buyer note.” Put only traceable information in the first: a size chart is present, the option menu shows a particular label, or the listing states a package quantity. Put opinions in the second: the material looks thin, the photographs seem inconsistent, or the design is preferable. Keeping these fields apart prevents an impression from becoming a remembered fact after you revisit the sheet.

Use a simple evidence status such as confirmed, unclear or missing. Avoid one total score unless every candidate is being judged on the same criteria. A low price should not cancel out a missing size chart, and attractive seller photography should not count as warehouse evidence. Our product-detail checklist can help you decide which listing fields deserve their own columns.

Do not turn the listed price into a delivered-price promise

Record the item price and any domestic delivery shown at the time of purchase, but label them as purchase-stage figures. PikoBuy’s guide says prices are subject to the actual purchase and that an out-of-stock order will be refunded. A spreadsheet should therefore have a “paid amount” field separate from an early observed price. Never silently overwrite the first figure; preserving both values makes the change visible.

International shipping comes later in the official workflow. PikoBuy’s shipping estimate asks for destination, product type, weight, length, width and height, while its guide says routes differ in delivery time and billing methods. Before the item reaches the warehouse, some of those inputs may be unknown. Mark them as unknown rather than inventing a landed total. Once parcel data and live routes are available, use a separate parcel tab and the process in our PikoBuy shipping guide.

Build a clean handoff into PikoBuy search

When a candidate survives the shortlist, copy the source link or precise keywords into PikoBuy. The official guide says the search box accepts product links or keywords, and the homepage also describes search by images or sourcing request. Keep the original source URL in the sheet even if PikoBuy produces its own product page. That gives you a reference for the offer you researched.

Before payment, compare the PikoBuy order screen with the row character by character: colour, size, quantity and version. Then record the order number or internal reference without storing passwords, full card details or unnecessary personal information. A spreadsheet shared with someone else should contain the minimum data needed for product research, not sensitive account credentials.

Change the row when the item enters the warehouse

The row should move from “candidate” to “ordered,” and later to “in warehouse.” PikoBuy says warehouse processing includes check-in, photo confirmation and a defect check. Add the warehouse status time, visible label, visible colour, counted pieces and links or filenames for the inspection images. Compare those images with the paid option and your saved listing evidence, not just with memory.

Add a “QC limit” field for questions the photos cannot answer. An image may show a label or visible stain, but it may not establish fit, internal operation, material composition or authenticity. PikoBuy’s Shipping Terms say professional inspection is unavailable for special and professional products and that buyers can view inspection photos or purchase additional detailed photos. Request another image only when a particular angle or close-up can resolve a visible uncertainty. Our QC photo guide gives a practical comparison sequence.

Let deadlines control the next-action column

A useful spreadsheet is not only an archive; it tells you what to do next. Add a deadline and next-action field for every warehouse item. If evidence shows a mismatch, note the exact issue and preserve the supporting images. PikoBuy’s current return page says eligible requests may be submitted within five days after the status changes to “In Warehouse,” defined as 120 hours from the next hour, subject to seller consent, resale condition and category rules.

Do not convert that statement into “all items have free five-day returns.” The policy lists exclusions and possible charges, including return delivery, the seller’s original shipping fee and an RMB 5 service fee for an unconditional return. Link your row to a factual reason and check the live policy for the individual product. The PikoBuy return guide explains the decision window in detail.

Use one parcel tab for consolidation decisions

Multiple orders can become one international parcel, but the product sheet and parcel sheet should remain separate. In the parcel tab, list which warehouse items are included, destination, recorded weight and dimensions, product type, packaging request, route considered, quoted amount and date checked. This structure prevents an old estimate for one group of items from being mistaken for a current quote on another parcel.

PikoBuy’s guide allows users to add requests such as minimal or reinforced packaging at warehouse inspection, then choose a route and pay the international shipping fee. Its Shipping Terms state that third-party logistics providers carry parcels and identify customs action, damage, loss and peak-season delays among cross-border risks. Your spreadsheet can document a choice; it cannot remove those risks or guarantee a delivery date.

A five-minute maintenance routine

At the end of a research session, remove dead candidates, stamp every checked row with the current date, and highlight missing evidence. Before ordering, reopen the source and confirm the exact option. After payment, lock the observed fields and add the actual order values. At warehouse arrival, attach the photo record and make a keep, clarify or return decision. At parcel submission, record the live route information separately.

This routine keeps the sheet small enough to trust. A giant collection of unexplained links creates the appearance of research while hiding stale information. Ten well-documented candidates are more useful than hundreds of rows without dates, specifications or evidence. The purpose is not to predict which item will be perfect; it is to make every assumption visible before it becomes expensive to correct.

What a responsible PikoBuy spreadsheet can and cannot do

A good sheet can preserve source links, make like-for-like comparison easier, show missing details, support warehouse-photo review and keep parcel calculations attached to the right inputs. It can help you notice that the option ordered differs from the option researched. It can also show which questions must be answered by the seller, PikoBuy or a carrier.

It cannot verify stock, seller reliability, product authenticity, fit, final shipping cost, customs treatment or delivery outcome. Those require current evidence from the responsible stage.
