LISTING RESEARCH GUIDE

Check the listing before you search.

A practical process for turning a vague shoe search into a precise research record—without presenting unverified product claims as facts.

1. Save the exact variant

Record the product title, selected colour, size, price shown, source link and date. Listings can change after you save them. “Black size 42” in a later result may not be the same product, material or option as the one you originally reviewed.

2. Keep different kinds of evidence separate

Use three labels: visible in the current listing, stated by the seller, and unknown. A current photo may show an outsole, a heel, a label or an accessory. It cannot prove fit, comfort, authenticity, material composition, durability or future availability. Keeping this boundary protects research from becoming an unsupported endorsement.

3. Compare measurements instead of size labels alone

Use stated measurements when available and compare them with footwear that fits you. Note the method where it is specified. A label is a starting point, not a universal fit prediction.

4. Search only after the question is specific

Once you know the model terms, selected option and visible attributes that matter, continue to the product database. Recheck every live result for its current variant, price and availability before making any later decision. Search results support discovery; the current listing controls the facts.
