Joyagoo Shoe Finds: Sizing and QC Checklist

Compare the exact shoe option, measurements and warehouse photos before the parcel is submitted.

Record the intended footwear

Choose the specific colour, version and quantity, then inspect the seller’s size chart and units. A familiar EU or US size is useful context but cannot guarantee that this particular design will fit. Save the option and listing while they remain available.

Compare the whole pair at the warehouse

Review both left and right shoes rather than relying on a single angle. Inspect the visible toe, heel, side, stitching, labels and sole. If an important view or measurement is missing, ask whether an additional image is available and what it costs.

Make the return and packing choices separately

If the pair appears different from the order or visibly damaged, contact the agent promptly and check current return conditions before shipping. After the issue is settled, weigh the protection of a box against its added volume and compare the actual parcel quote.

Match the measurement to the chart

Find out whether the seller means foot length, insole length or another dimension. Compare like with like using a comfortable pair and the same units. Measurement method and shoe shape can change the result; ask for a clear image next to a ruler if the difference is decisive.

Preserve the version selected

A product page can group several sizes, colours or versions under similar titles. Save the selected option, quantity and order confirmation before checkout. These records make it easier to compare the received pair even if the seller later changes the listing.

Examine both sides of the evidence

Look for visible differences between left and right shoes, including toe boxes, heels, sides, soles and labels. Check any stated accessories. Lighting and perspective can change appearance; if a side is hidden, request an appropriate view rather than treating the absence of evidence as a pass.

Know the limits of visual inspection

A QC image records what appears in the frame. It cannot establish authenticity, the composition of hidden parts, future durability or comfort on your foot. Use the images to frame specific questions, while recognising that a warehouse image is not a physical examination or performance guarantee.

Raise a problem within the applicable period

Identify the option ordered and the image showing the discrepancy, then ask the agent about clarification, exchange or return. Joyagoo describes a request window following QC completion. Verify the live deadline, seller eligibility and charges; a request does not guarantee an exchange or refund.

Choose protection for the actual parcel

A box can keep a pair and accessories together, while adding size and possibly billable weight. Removing it can increase compression risk; reinforcement can also alter dimensions. Review options and the quote for your destination, keep the parcel details, and document visible packaging damage on delivery.

Start with the shoe and your use

A useful shortlist begins with what the shoes need to do: everyday walking, a particular outfit or another specific use. Compare the shape, material description, closure and sole shown by the seller instead of relying only on a product name. Save the colour and version as well as the size, because similar photographs can lead to different options in a listing. If a feature matters, such as a removable insole or included spare laces, check whether the seller actually states it. An image alone may not show what is included or how a shoe will feel.

Measure a pair consistently

Place a comfortable shoe on a level surface and decide which measurement is relevant to the seller’s chart. If comparing insole length, measure the insole in the same way as the seller; if comparing foot length, do not substitute an exterior sole measurement. Note centimetres or millimetres and any measurement tolerance. Shape, width, padding and the way a shoe is made also affect fit, so length is a starting point rather than a promise. If the chart is ambiguous, ask for clarification before ordering and keep the answer with the product record.

Preserve what you selected

Before checkout, record the product link, seller, displayed price, size system, colour, version and quantity. Save the option screen or order confirmation, especially where several variants share one title. On warehouse arrival, use that saved record to compare the pair photographed. A different box label, accessory or displayed size may have an explanation, but it deserves a question before shipping. If the seller edits or removes the original listing, your own record is still useful for explaining the discrepancy to the agent.

Ask for a photograph that resolves a doubt

A useful request specifies the exact detail and why it matters: the size label inside each shoe, both heels together, the outsole, a close-up of a damaged seam or an insole next to a ruler. Check whether the warehouse offers the requested view, the applicable fee and any effect on timing. Remember that ruler placement, perspective and the measuring method can affect an apparent dimension. If a new image does not answer the question, ask for clarification rather than treating an unclear photo as a pass.

Respond to mismatches before parcel submission

If the delivered pair appears to have a different colour, size or visible defect, identify the selected option and the photo that shows the concern. Use the order’s support channel to ask about clarification, return or exchange, and check the current deadline, seller eligibility and any charge. JoyaGoo’s published guidance describes a five-day request window after QC completion, but the live terms and seller conditions control the individual case. A request is not a guaranteed refund. Resolve the question before putting the shoes in an international parcel.

Choose packaging with a clear trade-off

Original boxes, reinforcement and protective material can help prevent compression and keep accessories together, but may add weight or volume. Removing a box may reduce parcel size while increasing the chance of deformation. Compare the available options and actual quote for your destination rather than assuming one approach is always cheaper. Retain the order and parcel records. If the delivered package is visibly damaged, photograph the packaging and contents promptly and follow the platform or carrier’s current claims procedure. No QC photo can guarantee fit, authenticity or durability.

When the size chart and the photo disagree

Treat a disagreement as a question, not proof that either record is correct. Confirm whether the chart describes foot length, insole length or another dimension, and whether both values use the same unit. A photo may show a different angle or a measurement made along a curve. Ask the agent for the measurement method and a clear view of the ruler when that difference affects your choice. Check any optional photo cost before requesting it.

Record a decision, not just a verdict

After reviewing the pair, write down what you checked and any unresolved question. For example, the size label matches, both shoes are pictured, but an insole measurement is missing. Decide whether that missing information would actually change the purchase decision and request it if needed. Keeping this note beside the order is useful if you later contact support or need to compare the delivered item with the warehouse photos.

Compare after delivery without overclaiming

When the pair arrives, compare its size label, colour, visible condition and included accessories with the order and warehouse photos. Keep packaging and records while you check the current claim procedure if the parcel or contents were damaged. A difference in lighting can affect colour, and neither a photograph nor a first try-on proves long-term durability. Follow the platform or carrier’s actual deadlines and provide accurate details. Keeping the records together makes it easier to explain a concern without promising a refund or assuming that every issue was visible before dispatch.
