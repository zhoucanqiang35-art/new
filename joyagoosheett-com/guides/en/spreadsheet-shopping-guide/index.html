How to Use a Joyagoo Spreadsheet: Find, Check and Order Products

A spreadsheet is a discovery index. Check the live product listing and current options before paying.

Set the purpose of a find

Start with a product type, intended use and one or two features that matter. A directory can help narrow the search, but it does not sell the product or confirm that a seller still has stock. Use the relevant FindSpreadsheet category or search result to locate a current record.

Capture the choice before checkout

Write down the product address, seller, displayed price and the exact size, colour and quantity you intend to select. Similar photographs can represent different options. Keep a dated note or screenshot so you can later compare the submitted order against the choice you made.

Separate discovery from the agent order

On Joyagoo, check that the imported product and selected variant match the record you researched. The platform’s published instructions describe paying for the product and domestic delivery before the seller sends it to the warehouse. Check the current payment screen rather than assuming that a discovery-site price is a final invoice.

Recheck a record when circumstances change

A useful link can become stale when a seller edits an offer or changes sizes, prices or availability. Reopen the current listing instead of treating your saved note as a guarantee. If the record no longer shows the feature that mattered, look for clarification or compare another candidate.

Use the measurements appropriate to the item

A clothing measurement, shoe size label and electronic model number answer different questions. Match clothing dimensions to a comparable garment, examine the shoe seller’s sizing method, and check compatibility and shipping restrictions for electronics. Price alone cannot establish fit, function or the cost of a parcel.

Budget for the two stages

The first payment generally relates to the item and domestic delivery. International freight is considered later when you submit the parcel; optional warehouse services and destination charges may also apply. Track each part separately and rely on the actual offer for your order, not another shopper’s total.

Compare the received item with your selection

When warehouse images are available, place them beside the saved order confirmation. Check the visible variant, quantity and condition, then identify any view or measurement you need to decide. Confirm whether an additional photograph costs money. A photograph cannot prove authenticity, hidden materials, electronic operation or future durability.

Resolve a discrepancy while action is possible

If the photographed item appears wrong or damaged, describe the selected option and the specific difference through the order channel. Joyagoo’s published guidance describes a request period after QC completion; confirm the current deadline, seller eligibility and possible charges. A request does not guarantee a refund.

Finish the product decision before packing

Once each item is settled, choose among the routes actually offered for your destination. Review product restrictions, packaging protection, dimensions and the final quote before payment. Keep the order, warehouse images, parcel number and dated tracking events together, and provide accurate destination information.

Build a shortlist that you can revisit

A useful shortlist contains enough detail to explain why an item passed your first review. Record the category, a short description, product URL, seller, listed currency and date checked. Add the exact colour and size or a note saying that no suitable option is currently available. If you have several candidates, record a reason for each: for example, the measurements match a garment you own or the material description is clearer. This makes it easier to compare again after a listing changes. Avoid treating an old screenshot as proof of current stock; reopen the record before placing the order.

Check the destination of a product link

An old entry can fail for several reasons: the listing may have been removed, a variant may be sold out, a page may require a login, or the agent may not be able to import that particular source. Start by checking whether the link opens the expected item and whether the selected option is still available. If the title, pictures and variations no longer agree with your notes, pause and search for a current record. Keep the old URL in your notes for context, but do not assume a similar-looking replacement is the same item or seller.

Compare measurements consistently

Measurements are useful only when they describe the same thing. A flat chest width is not the same measurement as a body circumference, and an insole measurement is not always equal to foot length. Check units and how the seller took each measurement. Compare clothing with a garment you already like, laid flat in the same way; compare footwear with the relevant measurement of a comfortable pair. Allow for the seller’s stated measurement tolerance if one is provided. When a decisive measurement is absent, ask for it rather than selecting a size from a label alone.

Read the order confirmation as a separate record

The listing you researched and the order submitted through the agent may be viewed at different times. Before payment, compare the product image, variant, quantity, price, domestic delivery and seller notes with your shortlist. After payment, keep the confirmation and monitor the order status. If the seller cannot supply the chosen item, use the platform’s order channel to clarify the next step. Do not count an item as ready to ship until it has actually arrived and you have reviewed the available warehouse evidence.

Use QC photos for a specific decision

A warehouse photo is most helpful when compared with a question: is the colour the one ordered, is the size label visible, are both shoes present, or is a seam visibly damaged? Look at all available angles before concluding that an item is acceptable. If the detail is hidden, specify the angle or measurement you need and check the charge before requesting it. Photographs can document visible features, but cannot establish authenticity, hidden material, future durability or electronic performance. Save the order record and QC evidence while deciding whether to keep the item.

Close the loop before shipping

Set a reminder when QC is completed and check the current return or exchange window and seller conditions. Explain a discrepancy using the order option and the relevant image; ask what charges and refund rules apply before confirming. When every item is resolved, compare available routes, packing and the complete parcel quote. After dispatch, save the tracking number and dated events, and contact the platform if an issue requires investigation. This sequence keeps discovery, seller purchase, warehouse decisions and international shipping separate, so that a problem is addressed at the stage where action is still practical.

Plan a next step for each saved item

A shortlist is most useful when each row has a clear status: still comparing, ready to order, waiting for the seller, reviewing warehouse evidence, or resolved. Add the date when you last checked the record and the question that remains open. This makes it easier to spot stale links and avoid ordering the same variation twice. Once an item is resolved, keep the order number and move to parcel planning. If you cannot answer a decisive question, leave the item on the shortlist until the seller or agent provides enough information.

Keep a dated change log

If you compare the same listing over several visits, add a dated note when the available sizes, price, seller or delivery charge changes. Separate what the page says from an assumption you have not confirmed. That record helps you decide whether the same product still meets your needs and gives support a precise reference if a mismatch appears. Before paying, reopen the listing and verify the current option rather than relying on a previous screenshot. If a key detail cannot be checked, leave the item on your shortlist until it can be resolved.
