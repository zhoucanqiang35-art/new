How the PikoBuy workflow works: a practical guide to orders, warehouse checks and international shipping

This independent guide explains the steps PikoBuy publicly describes, plus the decisions an international buyer should make before paying. It is not affiliated with PikoBuy and does not promise availability, delivery, quality or refunds.

01

Start with the right expectation

PikoBuy presents itself as a China purchasing and forwarding service. In plain terms, that means a shopper can identify an item, ask the platform to purchase it, wait for it to reach a China warehouse, then create an international parcel. This is different from buying from one retailer that owns every part of the transaction. There can be a seller, a domestic delivery leg, a warehouse process and a separate international carrier. Each step has its own information and risk.

The useful way to approach the service is to separate what is known from what still needs checking. A product title and photo are not a confirmed size, stock level, material or final landed cost. A warehouse photograph can help you inspect what arrived; it cannot prove authenticity, long-term durability, fit, customs clearance or delivery success. PikoBuy’s own workflow notes that actual purchase prices can vary and that an out-of-stock order is refunded. That is why checking the chosen colour, size, quantity and seller information before the first payment matters.

02

Find an item, then record the exact variant

PikoBuy says shoppers can search on its site or submit a product link, image or sourcing request. Its beginner guide also names marketplaces such as Taobao, Tmall, 1688, Weidian and Yupoo as places where a shopper may first identify an item. The practical task is not merely “find a link.” Save the item title, link, seller, selected colour, selected size, quantity, listed domestic shipping terms and any notes that change the order.

Before submitting, read the size chart rather than assuming a familiar label has the same measurements. If an item has variants, compare the chosen variant against the seller’s photos and description. If the listing is unclear, treat it as unclear—do not turn a guess into a fact. Keeping a concise order note is useful if the seller later reports a stock or variant issue. For product discovery, the category pages on this site route only to the live FindSpreadsheet database; use the current record as a starting point and verify the live listing before purchase.

03

Understand the first payment

The public beginner guide describes selecting specifications, submitting the purchasing order and completing a first payment. It also says prices are subject to the actual purchase and that an out-of-stock order will be refunded. This does not mean every change is automatically resolved in the shopper’s preferred way. It means the shopper should keep enough context to assess any update: which item changed, whether the change is price, availability or specification, and whether an alternative is acceptable.

Do not treat an initial item price as the complete cost of an international purchase. Domestic seller shipping, warehouse services, return fees where applicable, parcel weight or dimensions, route selection, tax and customs charges can all affect the final cost. PikoBuy provides a shipping estimate page that asks for destination, product type, weight and parcel dimensions. A calculation is an estimate, not a guarantee. Use a realistic weight range and leave room in the budget for changes after items are received and packed.

04

Use warehouse photos as a decision point

PikoBuy describes warehouse inspection as check-in, photo confirmation and a defect check after items arrive. Its shipping terms say forwarded goods are unpacked and inspected, that inspection photos can be viewed, and that additional detailed photos may be purchased. This is the point where patience is most useful: compare the received item with the order you actually placed, not with an idealised image from a listing.

For clothing, check the visible colour, print placement, measurements if provided, labels, seams and obvious marks. For shoes, look at the pair, size marking, panels, soles and obvious damage. For electronics, confirm the exact model and visible accessories, but do not assume a photo proves function, compatibility or safety. Make any decision while the item is still in the warehouse. PikoBuy’s public returns page states that a return request for qualifying items must be made within five days after the status becomes “In Warehouse,” with eligibility also depending on seller rules and resalable condition.

05

Know the limits of returns

Returns are a common source of bad assumptions. PikoBuy’s returns page distinguishes between eligible and ineligible products. It lists examples that may not be returnable, including customised goods, second-hand goods, intimate apparel, food and certain opened or unsealed products. For an eligible product, the page says the seller must offer the return guarantee, the product must meet the seller’s resale condition and it must have been in the warehouse for no more than five days. The seller’s decision and applicable platform rules still matter.

Costs can also apply. PikoBuy states that a no-fault return can involve return shipping, the seller’s original shipping and a RMB 5 service fee. Where a seller is responsible—for example, a wrong item or a verified quality problem—the policy describes a different outcome, but it still says seller confirmation and the actual case matter. Read the current policy before relying on a return. Do not remove tags, seals, accessories or packaging if you may need to return an item.

06

Create a parcel with shipping risk in mind

Once goods are in the warehouse, the published guide says a shopper chooses a route, submits a parcel and pays international shipping. Route choice is not just a speed choice. Compare the destination, product restrictions, chargeable weight, parcel dimensions, tracking availability and any insurance terms offered at the time. PikoBuy says routes can differ in delivery time and billing method. The shipping estimate form itself asks for weight and dimensions, which is a reminder that bulky packaging can change the cost.

Its shipping terms also say international parcels are carried by third-party logistics providers and that cross-border risks, including customs policy changes, damage, loss, seizure and peak-season delays, cannot be eliminated. This is the honest planning point: do not order more than you can afford to have delayed or subject to import charges. Check the destination country’s current import rules yourself, use an accurate address, and keep invoices or order records where lawful and useful. No guide can promise customs treatment.

07

Track, document and decide carefully

PikoBuy’s guide says tracking information is available within three days after a parcel ships. That is a platform statement, not a delivery-time promise. A tracking number can take time to receive its first carrier scan, particularly around handovers. Save the parcel number, route name, declared shipment information shown in your account and warehouse photos. If something looks wrong, describe the specific order and evidence rather than sending a vague complaint.

Good purchasing decisions are deliberately unexciting: verify the variant, inspect the warehouse photos, understand whether a return is realistic, compare route constraints and budget for cross-border uncertainty. That approach will not remove risk, but it will reduce avoidable mistakes. Use this site for product discovery, then make decisions from the live listing and current official terms rather than from an old screenshot or a social-media claim.
