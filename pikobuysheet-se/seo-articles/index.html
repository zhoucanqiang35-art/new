PRACTICAL GUIDE · UPDATED SEPTEMBER 2026

How to use a buying-agent workflow without confusing research, inspection and delivery

A clear, fact-based walkthrough of the PikoBuy process: what happens before an item reaches the warehouse, what warehouse photos can tell you, and which decisions still belong to the buyer.

Scope: This independent guide summarises PikoBuy’s public home page, beginner guide, shipping terms, returns page and service terms. It is not an official PikoBuy page. Policies, routes and fees can change, so confirm the live terms before paying.

1. Begin with a product decision

PikoBuy’s public guide says shoppers can search by keyword, paste a product link, or use a sourcing request. It also says the shopper should confirm the item’s specification, colour and size before submitting an order. That first step prevents many costly mistakes: a different colourway, the wrong size option, an out-of-stock item, or a listing that does not match the page you saved.

Think of a listing as evidence to review, not a promise. Save the link, note the variation you selected, and keep the seller’s photos and measurements with the order details. If there is a size chart, compare it with an item you already own rather than relying only on the label. If an option is unclear, write a short, specific note before payment. The aim is to make the intended item clear enough that you can judge warehouse photos later.

PikoBuy describes its role as helping with product search, purchasing and payment assistance, inspection, and international shipping. Its terms also say it is generally not the manufacturer or seller of record. Product-level questions—material, fit, stock, seller claims and quality—still deserve the buyer’s own attention.

2. Separate the item payment from the parcel payment

PikoBuy’s beginner guide separates the purchase stage from the parcel-shipping stage. First, you submit the selected item and complete the initial payment for the purchasing order. When the item reaches the warehouse, you choose a shipping route, submit a parcel and pay the international shipping fee. These are distinct decisions. A low item price does not describe the final cost of getting a parcel to your address.

Before buying, make a simple budget with room for both stages. Include the item price, domestic delivery to the warehouse if applicable, return-related charges if you change your mind, international shipping, and possible duties, taxes, brokerage charges or other cross-border costs. PikoBuy’s terms say those cross-border charges are the customer’s responsibility. This is not a prediction that every order will incur them; it is a reason not to treat a displayed item price as a delivered price.

If the seller cannot fulfil the order, PikoBuy’s guide says an out-of-stock order is refunded. Keep item, shipping and customs questions separate so you can compare like with like before sending a parcel.

3. Use the warehouse stage as a visible checkpoint

PikoBuy’s public pages describe a warehouse stage with check-in, photos, inspection and repacking. The home page says items are inspected and repacked after arrival at the China warehouse; the beginner guide says the process includes photo confirmation and a defect check. Its shipping terms add an important boundary: inspection photos are available for review, and extra detailed photos can be purchased, but professional inspection is not available for special or professional products.

Use the photos for decisions they can support. Compare visible colour, size label, quantity, exterior condition, obvious stains, obvious damage and visible accessories against the order you intended to make. Look for enough context to identify the actual item, not just a close-up that could fit any variation. If a detail matters—such as a measurement, a tag or a specific included part—ask for an appropriate close-up before committing to international shipping.

Photos cannot guarantee authenticity, durability, comfort, internal condition, long-term performance or compliance with a seller’s marketing claim. Read them as a checkpoint: they reduce uncertainty about visible details, but they do not remove it.

4. Make the return window part of the warehouse review

Timing matters once an item is marked “In Warehouse.” PikoBuy’s returns page says a return application should be made within five days after that status, counted as 120 hours from the next hour after the status change. Eligibility depends on the seller offering the return guarantee, the item meeting the seller’s re-sale condition, and the item being within the warehouse window. PikoBuy also lists categories that may not qualify, including customised products, used products and intimate apparel; seller-specific rules can apply.

The useful habit is to review photos and order details promptly, not after you have assembled a large parcel. If you see a clear mismatch, read the eligibility conditions and choose a factual reason for the request. PikoBuy says return fees can include shipping back to the seller, the seller’s original shipping cost and a RMB 5 service fee for an unconditional return; responsibility can change how charges are handled. Do not assume a return is free just because an original listing advertised free domestic shipping.

For forwarded items, PikoBuy’s shipping terms say it does not provide after-sales service for the forwarded product itself; the buyer must contact the sender or seller, while PikoBuy can help ship goods back. Understand that distinction before using a forwarding order.

5. Estimate shipping with the variables that change the quote

PikoBuy provides a shipping-estimate tool that asks for destination country or region, product type, weight, and length, width and height. That tells you the core variables it uses for an estimate. Use the tool to compare possible parcel plans, not to promise a final delivered figure. Routes can bill differently, and PikoBuy’s beginner guide notes that routes differ in delivery time and billing methods.

For a useful estimate, start with a realistic destination, use the correct product type, and do not ignore dimensions. Then compare choices deliberately: one small parcel versus a combined parcel, a route that accepts your item type versus one that does not, and a budget route versus a route whose billing rules fit the parcel. Check current restrictions and estimates at the time you are ready to submit the parcel.

PikoBuy’s guide says tracking information becomes available within three days after shipment. That is not a delivery-time guarantee. Tracking can take time to appear, and cross-border delivery can be affected by carrier operations, customs processing, peak periods and other events outside the agent’s control.

6. Treat international shipping as a risk-managed process

PikoBuy’s shipping terms say parcels are carried by third-party logistics providers and that logistics risks must be assessed and borne by the user. The terms list customs policy, confiscation, damage, loss and peak-season delays as examples outside the platform’s control. This is the point where clear expectations matter more than optimistic language.

Before you pay for a parcel, check your destination’s import rules and whether the items are lawful to import. Give accurate name, address and contact details. Keep your order records, photos and tracking information. If your plan depends on a particular customs outcome, fixed arrival date or compensation result, pause and read current route information first. Responsible planning is not about assuming the worst; it is about not treating an international shipping service as if it were local retail delivery.

7. Use the service only for lawful, permitted goods

PikoBuy’s terms prohibit items that violate law, intellectual-property rights or its prohibited-items policy. It says the platform may cancel orders, refuse service, return or dispose of goods where violations occur, with related fees potentially borne by the customer. A product that cannot lawfully be purchased, exported, imported or transported is not made acceptable by a product link or an agent order form.

Use product research to find legitimate, permitted items and accurate information—not to bypass a brand owner’s rights, a seller’s restrictions, customs rules or a carrier’s prohibited-item rules. That approach makes the rest of the workflow easier to assess: the product description, payment record, photos and shipping documents all relate to goods you can reasonably expect to move through the process.

8. A practical pre-payment checklist

  1. Save the exact link and record the chosen size, colour, quantity and price.
  2. Read the seller’s item details and size information before the first payment.
  3. Budget separately for the item and international shipping.
  4. Review photos quickly after the item becomes “In Warehouse.”
  5. Check live return conditions before the five-day warehouse window expires.
  6. Use destination, item type, weight and dimensions when comparing estimates.
  7. Confirm that the item and shipping plan comply with applicable laws and route restrictions.
  8. Keep expectations realistic: photos and tracking are useful evidence, not guarantees.

When you are ready to compare live product information, use the database as a starting point and keep the final order decision tied to the current listing, policy and warehouse evidence.
