How to Use a USFans Spreadsheet: A Beginner’s Guide

A useful USFans agent guide starts before an order is placed. The official USFans homepage describes a simple public workflow: paste a product link to place an order, let the purchase move to the USFans warehouse, review QC and storage information, then select items from warehouse inventory for international shipment. That description is valuable because it separates the stages that many first-time shoppers wrongly blend together. Finding a product, buying it, receiving it at a warehouse, checking it and shipping it internationally are different decisions. A low product price does not settle the later questions about size, condition, storage, route availability or parcel cost.

Start with the exact listing. Save the seller link, variant, color, size, quantity and the price that is visible at the moment you research. If the platform accepts a pasted product link, use the real link rather than a screenshot or a vague product name. A link gives you a fixed reference point when you later compare the seller description with the warehouse photos. It also helps you identify whether you are looking at the same version of the product after a seller changes an option, replaces images or adjusts a price. For a spreadsheet shopper, this is the first discipline: one item, one source, one set of visible details.

The next step is to identify what the public USFans page actually promises and what it does not. USFans says it can assist with purchases from Taobao, 1688 and Weidian and describes buying products from China online and offline. It also says that products arrive at its warehouse before the international shipping stage. Those statements describe a service flow, not a guarantee that every seller, variant or destination will behave the same way. A research-minded buyer therefore checks the current listing and the current order screen, rather than assuming that a general homepage statement settles a particular purchase. Keep the seller information, your chosen variant and any stated restrictions together in one note before proceeding.

This approach is less dramatic than chasing a quick deal, but it is usually more useful. The goal is not to predict every outcome from a single product photo. The goal is to reduce avoidable surprises by creating a record you can compare at every stage: original listing, order choice, warehouse information and final parcel selection.
