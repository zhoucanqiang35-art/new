Litbuy Finds: Compare Products and Check Links

Start with the category that matches the item and open the current product listing. Record the seller reference, price, size chart, variant and the date checked. Similar photographs do not establish that two listings have the same seller, materials or specifications. Compare the actual options before making a decision.

If a link fails or the option disappears, return to the category and search again. Do not assume a visually similar listing is the same product. Verify current stock and pricing at the destination, and treat seller images and other buyers’ QC photos as references rather than proof of the exact item you will receive.

A useful product record distinguishes evidence from assumptions. Save the item ID, seller, selected option, displayed dimensions and price with a date. If a listing offers several versions, compare the exact option rather than using a headline image that may represent a different colour or size.

A previous buyer’s QC can help identify questions to ask, but it documents a different order at a particular time. Sellers can change stock and variants, and the available photo may not match your selection. Your own warehouse images should be compared with your saved order record when available.

When a link stops working, search the category or product description and review any new listing from the beginning. Confirm seller, variant, measurements and current price again. Keep an old reference as a note, not as proof that a replacement has the same origin or quality.

When comparing listings, make a short table for the same attributes: item identifier, seller, chosen option, stated measurements, displayed price and the date checked. Do not infer that two items are identical simply because their photos resemble one another or because their titles share a brand name.

A dead link can mean a listing was removed, an option sold out, or a temporary access problem. Search by the saved product description or category, open the new listing and confirm all details again. Never copy an old price or QC image onto a new product as though it proves equivalence.

Compare like with like. A low headline price may apply to one option, not the size or colour you need. Open the variant selector, check the corresponding price and availability, then save a dated reference. If a size chart is missing, ask for measurements instead of guessing from the product image.

Treat an older customer photo as historical evidence for a particular listing, not a guarantee of current stock. Sellers can change materials, options or photographs. If the product identifier, seller or variant differs, record it as a separate candidate and compare it independently.

Build a comparison table with the seller, item identifier, selected variant, listed measurements, price, domestic delivery and the date checked. A low displayed price may apply only to one option or exclude delivery. Open each candidate and confirm its current details before comparing totals. If two links use identical marketing photographs, that alone does not prove they come from the same seller, factory or batch; treat them as separate listings until supported by evidence.

A spreadsheet is a discovery aid, not an inventory guarantee. A row may include a product name, image, seller link and price, but it reflects the information available when somebody recorded it. Before ordering, open the current listing and confirm that it is still accessible, that the seller and product match the row, and that the option you want can actually be selected. Save the item identifier and the date checked. If the page is unavailable or the seller has changed the listing, treat the row as a lead for further research rather than evidence that the product remains available.

Compare equivalent variants. Two listings can show the same headline image while pricing different sizes, colours, quantities or versions. Select the intended option on each listing and record the resulting price, the seller’s measurements, the quantity and any domestic shipping cost. Check whether an apparent bargain is a deposit, a single component, a minimum-order price or a different model. If a detail is unclear, note it as unknown rather than assuming it matches. A proper comparison uses the same selection and the same destination for domestic delivery wherever that information is available.

Examine evidence by source and date. Seller photographs describe the listing; warehouse photos show the visible condition of a particular received item; a customer review is one person’s experience with a particular order. None proves that every future item will be identical. Look for useful details such as the option bought, measurement method and date, while treating promotional claims cautiously. If a review lacks a clear item reference or shows a different version, do not attach it to your candidate as proof. A large number of positive comments cannot establish authenticity or guarantee future performance.

Build a shortlist that can survive changes. For each candidate, keep a row with seller, item ID, variant, price, measurements, domestic shipping and date checked. Add a status such as available, unclear, unavailable or awaiting a response. Use the same units and note when a size chart uses body measurements rather than garment measurements. If the current listing contradicts an old spreadsheet row, rely on the current information for your decision and investigate the discrepancy. Avoid copying an outdated price into a new comparison without checking it again.

Calculate the expected cost beyond the displayed number. Add the selected item price and applicable domestic delivery, then consider service fees, international shipping and destination charges separately. The international cost depends on the parcel and cannot always be allocated accurately to one item before packing. For illustration, a $20 listing with $8 domestic delivery totals $28 before other costs, while a $25 listing with $2 delivery totals $27. These are examples, not live prices. A cheaper item is not automatically the better choice if measurements, quality evidence, conditions or availability differ.

Before submitting, check the return conditions and the route restrictions relevant to the actual product. Batteries, liquids and other goods can have special requirements that vary by route and destination. Ask the service about uncertain eligibility before placing an order. Keep your selected listing, option, price and measurement record so that warehouse photos can be compared with what you selected. Once the item arrives, verify the visible details and raise any specific concern while the item is still in the warehouse. Make the purchase and the parcel decision separately.

When a seller’s chart and another buyer’s measurements disagree, compare the size, measuring points and date before deciding which evidence is relevant. A chest width taken across a flat garment cannot be directly compared with a body circumference, and a review of another size may not answer your question. Request a current measurement if the difference matters to fit. If the item has multiple versions, record the product identifier and selected option with the measurement so the comparison is reproducible. A spreadsheet link can save research time, but it cannot replace this check. Review the seller’s current description and available return conditions as well. Where the listing, chart or delivery fee cannot be confirmed, keep that uncertainty visible in the table instead of filling the gap with a guess. Revisit the candidate when the missing information becomes available.
