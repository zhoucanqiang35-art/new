Litbuy Warehouse and Parcel Consolidation Guide

Items can arrive at different times. Keep an order record with the item reference, chosen variant, warehouse arrival date and inspection status. Check the current storage deadline and applicable terms in your account, because policies and individual order conditions can change.

Before combining items, resolve any visible discrepancy and check whether each item is eligible for the destination and route. Compare the final parcel weight, dimensions, packaging and live shipping quote. Save order IDs, QC images and tracking details; keep product decisions separate from the decision to submit a parcel.

Keep a small arrival table: item ID, variant, arrival date, inspection status and current storage deadline. An item awaiting a measurement should remain marked as unresolved. Review deadlines separately for each order and verify any charges or extension options in the current account terms.

Before combining, list the items and check the destination, route and item restrictions. Fragile or bulky goods may need protection that increases package dimensions; splitting a parcel may change costs and risks. Compare real quotes rather than assuming one parcel is always cheaper.

At submission, review the final recipient details, route, chargeable weight, dimensions and any selected protection. Keep the order and parcel IDs, photos and tracking number together. If a shipment is delayed or damaged, these records help you explain the issue under the applicable service terms.

Create a separate row for every order, including the day the warehouse received it, the current storage deadline, the photos reviewed and any unresolved question. Check each item’s deadline in the account, because arrivals may be days apart. A reminder is useful, but the live account remains the authority.

Before approving a combined parcel, resolve wrong variants and missing measurements while the items are still in the warehouse. Compare the cost and protection of one parcel against separate parcels using live quotes. Retain parcel IDs, packing choices and tracking records so that any later support request can identify the correct shipment.

As items arrive, review each order separately and mark it as waiting, inspected, needs clarification or ready. Record the actual deadline shown for that item; do not assume every order shares the same storage date. This makes it easier to decide whether to wait for another item.

Before submitting the parcel, check destination, recipient details, restricted contents and the actual packing options. Review the final chargeable weight and quote, then retain the order IDs, parcel ID and tracking number. If a problem occurs, provide these records to the service and follow the current policy.

Use a readiness gate before sending: every item has arrived, each inspection question is resolved or knowingly accepted, and the destination and route permit the contents. Then compare the full quote for one combined parcel with quotes for separate parcels. Note which scenario includes extra protection and whether fragile goods could be compressed by heavier items. Record the selected option and the final confirmed charges; an earlier estimate may change after weighing and packing.

Treat the warehouse as a sequence of separate decisions. A seller first sends an item domestically; after the warehouse records its arrival, you can review the available inspection information and decide what to do next. A parcel is a later decision involving destination, route, packaging and payment. The precise statuses shown in an account can change, so use the current order and parcel screens as the source of truth. Do not assume that an item marked as purchased has already arrived, or that two different orders were received on the same day. Their storage deadlines and inspection needs may therefore differ.

Create an arrival ledger with one line per order: seller or item ID, variant, arrival date, photos reviewed, open questions and the current storage deadline shown in your account. Mark an item as ready only when you have checked the visible details and resolved questions that matter to you. If a second item is still in domestic transit, check whether waiting for it is compatible with the first item’s deadline. Set a reminder before the earliest deadline, but confirm any storage charge or extension directly in the current service terms. Do not assume that all items share one timer.

Keep inspection and consolidation separate. A combined parcel is easier to prepare when each item has already been checked for the selected size, colour, quantity and visible condition. If a photo does not show a critical label or measurement, ask whether a clearer view is available and whether it costs extra. Discuss a discrepancy and any possible return with the service while the item remains in the warehouse. Available remedies depend on the seller and the applicable deadlines. Packing an unresolved item with others can make later questions harder to separate, so document what you accepted and what remains unknown.

Compare a combined parcel with separate parcels using the actual destination and contents. One parcel may reduce duplicated base charges, but it can also become bulky, exceed a route limit or need additional protection. Two parcels may permit different routes or packing, but can have separate base charges and tracking records. Create a scenario for each option and enter the live quote, chargeable weight, dimensions, included protection and relevant limits. Use the same item list and destination when comparing totals. Do not treat either option as universally cheaper; the result depends on the packed parcels and current route terms.

Consider packing by item and risk. A rigid box can protect a fragile object while adding size and weight; removing a box may save space but reduce protection or be unavailable for a particular item. Heavier goods can press on lighter items, so describe any separation or padding you need and check whether the service offers it and at what cost. Verify restrictions for batteries, liquids and other sensitive contents before choosing a route. Keep the packaging decision and its cost with the quote. A photo of an unpacked product cannot guarantee how it will perform during international transit.

Before paying for a parcel, review the recipient details, destination, exact item list, route, protection, final chargeable weight and dimensions. Check any declaration requirements and the current terms for loss or damage; do not assume that every option includes the same coverage. Save the parcel ID, item IDs, quote, payment record and tracking information. If delivery is delayed or damaged, document the issue and contact the service through its stated process within applicable deadlines. Retaining these records helps support identify the correct order, but it does not promise a particular outcome or override the service’s terms.

Use a simple timeline when orders arrive on different days. Imagine that item A is already inspected while item B is still travelling to the warehouse. Record A’s actual storage deadline from the account and check the expected status of B; then decide whether waiting is practical under the current terms. If a deadline is close, compare sending A separately against waiting only after checking any applicable fees and available routes. This example does not prescribe a storage period or guarantee an extension. Once B arrives, inspect it independently and update the comparison using current packed dimensions and quotes. Keep the reason for your decision in the ledger: it makes a later shipping charge or support question easier to understand. A reminder is useful, but the account’s current information and the service’s terms remain the reference.
