GUIDE 01 · LITBUY SPREADSHEET

How to use a LitBuy spreadsheet as a research tool, not a promise

A useful LitBuy spreadsheet reduces the time needed to find, compare and reopen a product record. It is not a shop counter, a seller guarantee or an inventory system. That distinction matters because a product card can remain helpful even when a seller changes a variant, adjusts a price or removes a listing. Treat the directory as the start of a decision. The live purchase route is where you confirm the current option and the current terms before money changes hands.

Begin with a written brief. “Black shoulder bag, narrow strap, fits a phone and glasses” is more useful than “bags.” Add what cannot change: material, approximate dimensions, colour, budget and intended use. Search using the stable part of the phrase, then use category routes to remove noise. A first pass should create a working set, not a winner. Three to eight candidates is usually enough to compare carefully.

For each candidate, capture four facts before looking at styling: category, displayed USD estimate, product ID and the image set on the detail page. The ID is especially valuable because titles can be translated, abbreviated or repeated. It gives you a stable handle for notes and for returning to the same subsite record. If two cards look alike but have different IDs, treat them as different listings until the live route proves otherwise.

Split what you see into evidence and assumptions. Evidence is a listed price, visible option name, attached image and date checked. Assumptions are “this will fit,” “the shade will match,” “the seller still has stock” and “shipping will be cheap.” Keeping those apart stops a tidy directory entry from becoming more certain than its underlying listing. Use a status label — investigate, compare, ready to verify or discard — so you know what work remains.

LitBuy publicly describes a purchase-to-warehouse stage and a later international-parcel stage. That makes a directory most useful when it supports both: use it to narrow a product choice, then use the live service to review the current order and parcel information. The best spreadsheet is not the one with the most links; it is the one that records enough context to make the next decision without starting research from zero.

Takeaway: use the spreadsheet to narrow and document choices. Use the live purchase page to confirm the current option, current price and policy.

GUIDE 02 · PRODUCT ID

What a LitBuy product ID can identify — and what it cannot confirm

A product ID is a routing key, not a certificate. In this directory it connects a catalog record to its matching LitBuy purchase destination. That makes it more dependable than a shortened title for returning to the same item, sharing a candidate or recording a decision. It does not prove that a seller has stock, that every option uses the same materials or that the product shown in a photo is the unit you will receive.

Use the ID at the beginning and end of a review. Copy it into your notes next to the date, then compare it with the live product route just before buying. If the title looks similar but the ID differs, pause. A small difference can mean a different seller, replacement listing or variant family. Never rely on an image alone when there are look-alike products.

A clean note has five fields: product ID, title as shown, chosen option, visible source-image count and last-checked date. If the page later changes, you can describe what you saw instead of relying on memory. For size-sensitive items, add the size-chart reference and the measurement you need. For colours, save the option label rather than your own shorthand; labels make later comparison easier.

Do not turn an ID into an authenticity signal. LitBuy’s public product-page notice says it supplies search, purchasing and forwarding for third-party-platform goods and cannot verify product quality or authenticity. “This ID matches my notes” is useful. “This ID proves the item is genuine” is not supported. Keep claims limited to what the identifier actually does.

The ID remains useful after ordering: it helps you compare a warehouse photo with the original source images or explain a question to support. But it is not an order number, parcel number or proof of delivery. Record those separately when the platform supplies them. This small distinction keeps a research log accurate through every stage.

Takeaway: a product ID identifies a route and improves record-keeping. It does not validate stock, quality, authenticity, sizing or final cost.

GUIDE 03 · IMAGE EVIDENCE

Weidian images, QC images and the difference between a listing picture and evidence

Images make a product feel certain quickly. They can also be overread. The top image series on this detail page is the original merchant-image set associated with the listing. It helps you learn the advertised design, colour range, trim and included accessories. It is not proof that a particular unit has been packed, and it cannot show hidden construction, accurate weight or how an item feels in daily use.

QC images have a different role. LitBuy publicly says it provides inspection photos when items arrive at the warehouse. Those images can be stronger evidence because they relate to a received item, but they still need careful reading. Ask what the image actually shows: front view, label, hardware, measurement, packaging or a defect. A single close-up is not a complete inspection. A sequence can help you spot obvious differences without becoming a guarantee about performance or authenticity.

This subsite keeps the distinction visible: selectable top thumbnails are merchant images, while the long gallery below prioritises QC images. If the imported record has no QC images, it falls back to the same merchant-image series instead of inventing an inspection set. Read the captions and context before assuming an image is warehouse evidence.

Compare with a checklist. Check silhouette first: seams, panels, pockets and closure placement. Then check scale using genuine dimensions, then surface details such as texture, zipper pulls and colour boundaries. Last, list what is not visible. The unanswered list is more useful than a rushed yes/no judgment. LitBuy’s notice that quality and authenticity cannot be verified should be read alongside its QC-photo service statement.

If authentic origin is essential, use an authorised retail channel rather than inferring certainty from images. QC photography can document visible condition and conformity to a chosen option; it cannot certify origin, composition, durability or future performance. That is not a flaw in the photograph — it is the limit of the evidence.

Takeaway: listing photos show what is advertised; QC photos can document what arrived. Neither supports an unsupported authenticity claim.

GUIDE 04 · OPTIONS & SIZING

How to verify colour, variant and sizing before you create a purchase request

Most avoidable purchase mistakes happen in the option selector, not in the hero image. Sellers commonly use one photo set for several colours, sizes, materials or bundles. The title may name a range while the option list contains the actual decision. Before buying, record the exact option label shown on the live product route. “Small black, matte hardware” is far more useful than “black bag.”

Ask whether every option uses the same product photo, whether price changes by option and whether the name refers to colour, size, version or quantity. If wording is unclear, do not resolve it by guessing from a thumbnail. A thumbnail can be reused or colour-shifted by lighting. Record the uncertainty and choose a clearer listing when the detail is decisive.

For clothing, compare measurements rather than familiar letter sizes. Measure a garment that already fits, then compare shoulder, chest, length, waist, rise and inseam with the seller’s chart. For footwear, use a measured foot length when possible. For bags, note width, height, depth, opening size and strap drop. A styled photo cannot answer whether your practical essentials fit.

Use a message field only for a precise factual request if it is available, and do not assume a message guarantees a seller change or response. Keep wording specific: “Please confirm option B, size 38” is clearer than “send the good version.” Save the option text and the time you checked it; availability can change between research and payment.

Size, colour and configuration remain live information. The spreadsheet can help you retain the question, but only the live route shows the current selector. Make the final check immediately before payment, and treat an absent measurement as unknown rather than as a likely fit.

Takeaway: choose from exact option labels and measurements, not from assumptions created by shared photos or familiar size letters.

GUIDE 05 · COMPARISON

How to compare similar listings without letting the lowest price decide everything

When several listings look alike, price is an invitation to investigate, not a verdict. A lower number may represent a smaller size, different material, partial bundle, different seller or an old cached price. Make candidates comparable first: use the same option, similar dimensions, similar stated material and similar quantity. Without that work, the difference is not meaningful.

Create a small comparison card: product ID, exact option, displayed USD estimate, image count, key measurements, stated material, visible inclusions and last-checked date. Then write one open question. “Lower price, but no dimensions shown” is more useful than “maybe.” It exposes uncertainty instead of hiding it inside a favourite image.

Do not reward polished photography automatically. Source images can be edited, shared among stores or shot under flattering lighting. Look for internal consistency: do title and option labels agree, do dimensions match the silhouette, and are key details repeated across angles? A less stylish listing with clear facts may be easier to evaluate than a glossy one with little information.

Keep price and later parcel cost in different fields. LitBuy describes a domestic purchase stage and a separate international-parcel stage, while its estimator requests destination, weight and dimensions. A product-price saving can be outweighed by parcel characteristics or selected services. Until the live route provides numbers, say “unknown” rather than inventing a total.

The best choice is often the option with the clearest information, not the lowest starting figure. If its configuration is explicit, its dimensions answer your need, its images let you inspect key features and its remaining uncertainty fits your budget, it may be the lower-risk candidate.

Takeaway: compare like with like, record missing information and keep item price separate from the later international-parcel decision.

GUIDE 06 · QC REVIEW

A practical QC workflow: inspect what matters before you submit an international parcel

Quality-control photos work best when you decide in advance what they need to answer. Save the merchant image that best shows the feature you care about, then write a short acceptance list: correct option, broad colour, visible dimensions where possible, key hardware and included parts. The list should be specific enough to guide a review, but modest enough not to imply an impossible guarantee.

When inspection images are available, establish identity first. Compare the item with the product ID, option notes and broad silhouette. Then work from large features to small details: general model, colour family, major pockets, closures and straps before stitching, texture or print alignment. A structural mismatch — visibly wrong size, missing component, broken closure or wrong option — deserves more attention than a minor lighting difference.

Photographs have blind spots. They may not show smell, feel, internal reinforcement, exact weight, durability or every side of an item. Absence of a flaw in a photo is not proof that no flaw exists; a faint mark can be shadow or compression. If the decision depends on a feature that is not visible, treat it as unresolved rather than granting it a pass.

Keep any support request factual: identify the item, expected option, observed difference and supporting image. Avoid claims about authenticity unless you have independent evidence and a policy that addresses it. LitBuy says it cannot verify quality or authenticity of third-party goods, so QC should focus on visible condition and conformity rather than certification.

LitBuy’s published flow separates warehouse arrival from later international shipping. That creates a sensible checkpoint: review the evidence available while the item is in storage, understand the next parcel decision, and submit only when the remaining uncertainty is acceptable to you.

Takeaway: QC is a feature-specific review of visible evidence. It cannot promise hidden quality, long-term performance or authenticity.

GUIDE 07 · SHORTLISTING

Build a shortlist from categories and search without losing the reason you saved each item

Browsing is easy; a useful shortlist is deliberate. Use categories when you know the object type and keyword search when you know a material, shape or functional feature. Save a candidate only when it passes a relevance test: correct category, enough image context, no obvious title contradiction and a product route you can verify later. A hundred attractive cards are not a hundred purchase-ready choices.

Use three tiers. Tier one is the active shortlist: no more than five items you would genuinely consider. Tier two is the comparison reserve: alternatives that need information. Tier three is inspiration: styles you like but cannot evaluate yet. This prevents a browsing session from becoming a misleading wishlist where everything looks equally ready.

Give every active item one clear reason for being there: stated measurements, a more useful image set, a specific function, or a comparable price. If you cannot explain it in one sentence, move it to reserve. That simple rule removes impulse saves and makes later comparison faster.

Local search and category routes are a map, not a substitute for current availability. This subsite keeps discovery inside the subsite; the purchase button is the deliberate point where you re-check live information. Mark records “checked today,” “needs live verification” or “archive.” A small current list beats a large stale list.

The purpose of a shortlist is not urgency. It is to preserve attention for measurements, option labels, image evidence and budget. That is what turns a spreadsheet from a gallery into a decision tool.

Takeaway: preserve the reason an item made your list, limit active candidates and archive stale records instead of treating them as live stock.

GUIDE 08 · TOTAL COST

Plan a LitBuy purchase budget without pretending the listing price is the final price

A displayed USD figure is useful for comparing products, but it is not a complete landed-cost quote. LitBuy describes a domestic purchase-to-warehouse stage and a later international-parcel stage. Its freight estimator asks for destination, weight, product type and dimensions. That is clear evidence that responsible shipping planning cannot be based on a product image or base price alone.

Build a budget in layers: exact item price, any domestic order charge shown at checkout, the later international-parcel estimate, and a contingency for destination taxes, payment conversion or optional services where applicable. Do not fill gaps with invented fixed fees. Use “unknown” until the live service provides a figure.

Weight and volume matter. Two similarly priced products can have very different parcel economics if one is bulky, rigid or packed in a large box. Take dimensions seriously for footwear, luggage, outerwear and fragile goods. An estimate remains an estimate, but it is better evidence than assuming a low item price stays low after international shipping.

LitBuy publicly advertises up to 120 days of storage and states that the first 90 days are free. Its Help Center contains the current storage-period information. Treat that as a live policy: verify it at the time you use the service, including any rules after the free period. Storage is useful, but it should not become an excuse to postpone a realistic parcel budget indefinitely.

If an item only works at the lowest imaginable shipping cost, it is not within budget yet. If it still works after honest uncertainty, it is a stronger candidate. A layered budget is less exciting than a single low price, but it prevents the costly mistake of buying before understanding what remains unknown.

Takeaway: record product price, but decide using a layered budget that leaves unknown costs honestly unknown until the live route provides them.

GUIDE 09 · RESPONSIBLE BUYING

A responsible pre-purchase checklist for third-party marketplace goods

Responsible buying begins with knowing who is making each claim. A third-party seller supplies the listing; a purchasing and forwarding service may handle order and parcel steps; customs authorities control what can enter a destination. These roles are not interchangeable. LitBuy’s product notice says it provides search, purchasing and forwarding for third-party-platform goods and cannot verify quality or authenticity.

Check legality before style. LitBuy publishes a prohibited-items statement and its product pages can show risk notices for restricted goods. Read current platform rules and your destination country’s import requirements. Do not try to bypass restrictions with misleading descriptions, undervaluation requests or false declarations. A spreadsheet can organise research; it cannot make an item lawful to ship or guarantee customs clearance.

Take extra care with branded, regulated, battery-powered, liquid, food, medical and fragile items. The right question is not “can I find a link?” but “is this permitted, and can it be transported safely and legally to my destination?” If the answer is unclear, choose a compliant alternative or consult an appropriate official source. Forum stories are not proof of a current rule.

Protect your account too. Register only on the official LitBuy domain, use a unique password and review the information you submit. The registration page asks for an email account, and the published privacy notice describes collection of account and contact details required for account use. Never send passwords, payment credentials or identity documents through a spreadsheet comment, social message or unverified link.

Before payment, read live cancellation, return, insurance and compensation terms that apply to your order. Keep language honest: do not market a third-party item as authentic because it resembles one, and do not describe a warehouse image as independent certification. Accurate language protects other buyers and makes your own records more useful.

Takeaway: respect import rules, secure your account, read current terms and keep claims limited to what the platform and visible evidence actually support.

GUIDE 10 · PURCHASE LOG

Keep a purchase log that survives listing changes, warehouse checks and shipping decisions

A purchase log is the bridge between research and action. Keep product ID, title, category, directory URL, purchase-route URL and date checked. Add the exact chosen variant, displayed price and why you selected it. This prevents reopening a familiar image later and forgetting which size, colour or version you actually intended to buy.

Make the log chronological. Add a line when you verify the live listing, when you pay, when the item reaches the warehouse and when you submit an international parcel. Keep dates separate from estimates: expected warehouse arrival and actual warehouse arrival are different facts. LitBuy’s own public flow distinguishes domestic purchasing from the later parcel step, and your notes should preserve that boundary.

Store evidence so you can understand it later: original listing screenshot, option screenshot, QC images if provided, message copies and order confirmation. Name files with product ID and date. If something changes, a dated record is more useful than memory. Do not edit a screenshot to create stronger evidence; retain the original and label any crop.

Use clear states: researching, live-verified, paid, warehouse review, ready to submit, shipped, delivered and closed. A product can be paid but not at the warehouse; at the warehouse but not ready for international submission; or delivered with an issue still open. Precise states tell you what action is actually next.

Record money by stage as well: expected item price, paid item price, parcel charge and any tax or fee actually paid. Over time this becomes your personal evidence base for future budgets. It is more reliable than generic claims about what a category “usually costs,” because it relates to your destination, parcel and payment date.

Takeaway: record product ID, option, dates, images, costs and status by stage. A precise log is the best defence against stale listings and vague memory.
