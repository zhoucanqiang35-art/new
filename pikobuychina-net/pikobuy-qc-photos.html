How to Use PikoBuy QC Photos: A Practical Warehouse Check Guide

Warehouse photos are useful decision evidence, not a promise about authenticity, durability or delivery. Here is a focused way to compare what you ordered with what arrived before you choose international shipping.

Independent research · reviewed 3 September 2026

When buying from China through a purchasing agent, the most important moment is often not the first checkout. It is the short period after an item reaches the warehouse and before it is included in an international parcel. At that point, you can compare the original listing with pictures of the received item and decide what to do next. The point is not to search for a vague verdict such as “good QC.” It is to check whether the visible evidence matches the exact order you made.

PikoBuy’s public guide says that, after an item reaches its warehouse, the process includes check-in, photo confirmation and a defect check. Its shipping terms say forwarded goods are unpacked and inspected, that inspection photos can be viewed, and that additional detailed photos may be purchased. Those statements define a helpful but limited role for the images: they can help you verify visible details. They cannot establish every fact a buyer might want to know.

Start by saving the order evidence

QC photos are only useful if you have something precise to compare them with. Before paying for the item, save the original product URL, seller name, title, selected colour, size, quantity and product images. Capture the size chart and any important listing note as well. A product page can change after an order is placed, and a search result or a headline photo may not show the exact variation chosen.

This is especially important for listings with many versions. “Black,” for example, can mean different shades or different product batches; a shoe listing may have multiple size systems; a bundle may contain a different number of pieces by option. Write an order note that states the actual option rather than relying on memory. A short note such as “navy / M / two pieces / check size label” is much easier to compare than an open-ended request to “check quality.”

What the official warehouse step can show

According to PikoBuy’s public descriptions, warehouse photos are part of receiving and inspection. That makes them useful for the questions a photograph can answer: Does the outer packaging display the expected seller or item label? Is the visible colour broadly consistent with the selected option? Are the expected pieces present? Is there an obvious stain, tear, missing part or different size label? For a set of clothing, images can help count the visible items. For footwear, they can help compare the size label and the two shoes. For an accessory, they can help identify the visible model or colour.

Use careful language when you make that comparison. A photo can show a visible mark; it cannot prove that no other mark exists. It can show the label in the picture; it cannot guarantee a fit. It can show apparent construction; it cannot prove materials, long-term durability, safety or authenticity. Light, camera angle and image compression can also change how colour and texture appear. Treat the photo as evidence to examine, not a certificate.

Use a category-specific checklist

One generic checklist is better than none, but the best questions depend on the item. For clothing, compare the visible colour, print placement, size tag, attached accessories and obvious marks. If the listing makes a measurement particularly important, ask for an image that helps verify it rather than assuming the listed size will fit. For shoes, compare the size label, left-and-right pair, visible outsole, laces and any supplied extras that were part of the order. For bags or accessories, look for the colour, model, closure, straps and included pieces shown in the listing.

For electronics or specialised items, be even more restrained. PikoBuy’s shipping terms say professional inspection cannot be provided for special and professional products. A warehouse image may confirm a model label or whether an item is visibly present, but it does not establish performance, compatibility, safety or internal condition. If the product needs a functional test or professional assessment, plan around that limitation before you buy.

Ask for a photo that answers one question

Additional images are most valuable when they resolve a specific uncertainty. “Please take a clear photo of the size label” is actionable. “Please show the two items together” can confirm quantity. “Please photograph the visible mark on the front” asks for evidence that can be compared with the original listing. By contrast, “Is it good?” depends on an undefined standard and may not give you a useful record.

Make the request short and factual. State the location or feature, then explain what you want shown. Do not ask a warehouse worker to declare whether an item is authentic, to assess a trend claim or to promise it will arrive safely. Those questions cannot be responsibly settled by a photo. The purpose is to reduce a visible uncertainty before the order becomes an international shipment.

Separate a visible mismatch from a preference change

A useful QC decision distinguishes between what the evidence shows and what you now prefer. If the received size label differs from the selected option, an expected part is missing, or a clear visible defect appears, preserve the relevant screenshot and photo. Compare both records before you contact support or request a return. If the item matches the selected option but you no longer like it, that is a different situation and may have different costs or eligibility.

PikoBuy’s current Returns & Exchanges page says that applications under its five-day return guarantee may be submitted within 120 hours after an order changes to “In Warehouse.” It also says eligibility depends on the seller offering the guarantee, the item meeting the seller’s resale condition, and the item being in the warehouse for no more than five days. Its page lists several categories that may not be returnable, including customised goods, second-hand goods, intimate apparel and food. This is why a prompt, evidence-led decision matters. Check the current policy and the seller’s terms for the exact item rather than assuming all products qualify.

Do the QC decision before you submit a parcel

International shipping introduces a new layer of cost and uncertainty. PikoBuy’s public guide says users select a shipping route and pay the international shipping fee after warehouse receipt. Its shipping terms say parcels are carried by third-party logistics providers and identify risks such as customs action, damage, loss and peak-season delay. Once an item is in a dispatched parcel, resolving a seller-side issue is usually more complicated than when the item is still in the warehouse.

So do not wait for a parcel to be consolidated just because the photo review feels inconvenient. First check the order evidence. Then review the available pictures. Request an additional detail photo only if it can resolve a defined issue. Finally, decide to keep the item or take the next step while the warehouse window remains open. For shipping planning, use the live estimate for the actual destination and final parcel information; PikoBuy’s estimator asks for destination, product type, weight, length, width and height, which is a reminder that the finished parcel—not only the product price—affects the decision.

A calm way to use QC photos

Good QC is not about hunting for perfection in every image. It is a practical check: retain the original listing, inspect the exact option, look for visible mismatches, make one clear photo request when necessary, and choose before international shipping. That approach avoids both extremes—treating every minor visual difference as conclusive, or ignoring evidence until the parcel has already left the warehouse.
