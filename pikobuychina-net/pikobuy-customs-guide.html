PikoBuy Customs Guide: Duties, Declarations and Restricted Items

Customs preparation begins before checkout. Identify the real product, preserve its value evidence and check destination rules before it becomes an international parcel.

Independent research · reviewed 17 September 2026

A useful PikoBuy customs plan starts much earlier than the shipping screen. When you buy from a Chinese marketplace for delivery abroad, the product description, materials, quantity, purchase evidence and destination can all affect whether the order is practical to ship. PikoBuy’s public Terms of Service also make the buyer responsible for duties, taxes, brokerage fees and other possible cross-border charges.

This guide does not calculate a tax rate or decide how a customs authority will classify a particular item. Those details depend on the destination, product and current law. Instead, it provides a factual preparation method based on PikoBuy’s published workflow: research the item, buy the exact option, review it at the warehouse, choose an available international route and preserve an evidence file for the parcel.

Start with the exact item, not a broad category

“Clothing,” “electronics” or “accessories” may be enough for browsing, but they are not enough for serious cross-border planning. Record what the product actually is, what it is made from, how it is used, its model or capacity, the number of pieces and whether it contains a battery, liquid, powder, food, medical component or branded material. Do this before ordering, while the seller page and selectable options are still visible.

PikoBuy’s beginner guide tells buyers to confirm specifications, colour and size on marketplaces such as Taobao, Tmall, 1688, Weidian and Yupoo. Preserve the seller, source link and exact option as well. Our PikoBuy product search guide explains how to keep those details attached to the order rather than relying on a translated title or a single product image.

Separate PikoBuy rules from destination law

There are several different gates. PikoBuy can decide whether it will accept or handle an item under its policies. A logistics provider can apply route-specific restrictions. Export and destination authorities apply their own laws, documentation rules and import controls. Passing one gate does not prove that an item will pass every other gate.

PikoBuy’s Terms say users must not request, list, purchase, ship or receive products that violate its prohibited-items rules, including products without lawful intellectual-property rights or authorisation. The same Terms state that the platform may cancel orders, refuse service, return or dispose of items when violations are found, with related fees, fines, storage and disposal costs borne by the customer. Screen the product before payment rather than treating warehouse receipt as permission to import it.

Avoid counterfeit and intellectual-property risk

PikoBuy publishes a zero-tolerance position on counterfeit goods and products that infringe intellectual-property rights. Its Terms describe measures including inspections, requests for documentation, listing removal, account suspension or termination and cooperation with rights holders or authorities. A familiar logo, seller claim or marketplace listing is not evidence that a product is lawfully authorised.

If lawful brand status matters, keep evidence from an authorised source and do not substitute social-media comments for documentation. Avoid listings that obscure trademarks, promise evasive shipping methods or ask for a false description. This site does not authenticate products. Warehouse photographs can help compare visible details, but they cannot transform an infringing item into a permitted one.

Build a truthful product description

A practical description names the object and its important characteristics in plain language. “One cotton T-shirt, adult size M” is more useful than “gift.” “USB keyboard with cable” is more useful than “computer item.” Do not copy promotional claims that do not identify the goods, and do not hide batteries, liquids, brand markings or multiple units behind a vague label.

The description should remain consistent across the seller order, warehouse record and parcel documents. If the seller title is inaccurate or machine-translated strangely, use the warehouse photos and source listing to clarify it before parcel submission. Resolve an identity mismatch while the item is still in China, when the evidence can be checked and a return may still be possible.

Preserve value evidence instead of guessing

Keep the order confirmation, amount actually paid, selected option, quantity, seller invoice if available and proof of any seller discount. If several items are combined, retain a line-by-line purchase record rather than only a single total. PikoBuy’s beginner guide separates the first product payment from the later international shipping payment, so your file should separate those amounts too.

Do not invent a lower figure or ask anyone to misdescribe the goods. A truthful evidence file is easier to explain if a carrier, broker or customs authority requests support. It also makes the landed-cost calculation clearer: product payment, domestic seller freight, international shipping and destination charges are different amounts with different purposes. The PikoBuy spreadsheet guide provides a simple way to maintain that ledger.

Use the warehouse checkpoint carefully

PikoBuy says its warehouse process includes check-in, photo confirmation and a defect check. For forwarded goods, its shipping terms say items must be unpacked and inspected, while professional inspection is unavailable for special and professional products. Review the photographs for evidence that affects customs preparation: quantity, model label, visible material claims, battery markings, package contents and brand presentation.

Inspection photographs have limits. They may not prove chemical composition, internal components, genuine licensing or technical compliance. If a visible label or measurement would answer a specific question, request a focused image before shipping. Use our PikoBuy QC photo checklist to distinguish what a photo shows from what remains unverified.

Check the destination before choosing a route

Import rules are destination-specific and can change. Research the current government and carrier requirements for the receiving country or region using the exact product details you collected. Check whether personal import is allowed, whether quantity appears commercial, whether permits or certifications are required and whether the selected logistics route accepts the product type.

PikoBuy’s shipping estimator asks for destination, product type, weight and parcel dimensions. That makes it a route-planning input, not a customs ruling or tax calculator. PikoBuy’s beginner guide also says routes differ in delivery time and billing methods. An available estimate should not be treated as a promise that customs will release the parcel or that no destination charge will apply.

Budget for duties, taxes and brokerage separately

PikoBuy’s Terms explicitly state that cross-border transactions may incur duties, taxes, brokerage fees or other charges and that the buyer is responsible for them. No single percentage can be honestly applied to every PikoBuy order. The amount can depend on the destination’s rules and the product information used by the relevant authority.

Keep a customs reserve rather than budgeting only the item and international shipping quotes. When comparing two products, consider whether one needs additional documentation or creates a higher compliance risk. A cheap item that cannot be lawfully imported is not a bargain. Likewise, a shipping estimate is not the final landed cost. Our PikoBuy shipping guide explains how to keep route pricing separate from destination-side costs.

Understand who carries logistics risk

PikoBuy’s shipping terms say parcels are carried by third-party logistics providers. They describe customs action, confiscation, damage, loss and peak-season delay as cross-border risks outside PikoBuy’s control and say the buyer must assess and bear logistics risks. This published limitation is a reason to document the parcel, not a prediction that any particular outcome will occur.

Before paying for dispatch, save the route name shown in your account, the submitted parcel contents, the destination address, packaging request and available protection information. After shipment, keep the tracking record and any official notice. PikoBuy’s beginner guide says tracking information will be available within three days after dispatch, but tracking availability is not a delivery guarantee.

Do not combine items blindly

Consolidating several purchases can simplify one part of the process, but different products may have different route or import constraints. Review the combined contents as one parcel before submission. A battery, liquid, branded item or regulated product can change the practical options for goods that would otherwise be straightforward.

Use warehouse weight and dimensions when available, and confirm any packaging request before comparing routes. PikoBuy’s guide gives minimal and reinforced packaging as examples of requests, while its estimator accepts weight and three dimensions. Packaging can protect goods and affect parcel size, but it does not remove the need for accurate product information or destination checks.

Know when to stop before international shipping

If you discover that the item is prohibited, unsupported by an acceptable route or unlikely to meet destination requirements, do not submit it for international shipping merely because it reached the warehouse. Keep the evidence and investigate the available return or disposal choices through the account. For agency purchases, timing may matter.

PikoBuy’s return policy says eligible applications can be made within five days after the status becomes “In Warehouse,” defined as 120 hours from the next hour. Seller consent, resale condition, category exclusions and possible fees also apply. Read the PikoBuy return policy guide promptly; forwarded products have the separate seller-contact boundary explained in the forwarding guide.

A pre-shipment customs-readiness checklist

Before creating the international parcel, confirm the destination address; plain-language product description; material, model and quantity; battery or liquid status; brand and authorisation evidence where relevant; amount actually paid; seller order; warehouse photographs; available route; and current destination requirements. Keep these facts consistent instead of preparing them only after a question arises.

Then separate confirmed facts from estimates. The paid product price is a record. Warehouse weight and dimensions may be measurements. A route quote is an estimate until the parcel is processed. Duties and other destination charges may remain unknown. Clear labels prevent an estimate from becoming a false promise in your budget.

The practical view of PikoBuy customs preparation

PikoBuy connects product purchasing or forwarding, a China warehouse and international logistics, but the buyer still needs to understand the product and destination. The strongest customs file is simple: an exact item record, truthful value evidence, visible warehouse confirmation, current route information and destination research performed before dispatch.
