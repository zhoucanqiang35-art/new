LoloBuy spreadsheet guide: find and check products before ordering

A spreadsheet can help you discover a product, but a saved listing is a starting point, not a guarantee. Compare the current product information, record the exact option and plan for costs beyond the item price before deciding whether to order.

What a spreadsheet can do for you

A product directory gathers links and basic details so that you can compare possible finds. Start with a category, such as shoes, hoodies or jackets, then narrow the search using the features that matter to you: colour, measurements, material description, product type and budget. A useful shortlist is small enough that you can inspect each listing carefully.

A directory card may reflect an earlier price, picture or description. It does not reserve inventory and does not establish that the seller's claims have been independently tested. Open the current product record and treat unavailable or conflicting information as an open question. If a listing no longer matches the card, use the live information and search again rather than guessing which option was intended.

Compare the listing and the selected option

Look beyond the headline image. Check the item name, the seller's option names or codes, quantity, colour and size. A single listing can contain several versions; an attractive price or photograph may belong to a different choice. Check whether the listed price is for one item, a set or an accessory, and whether the selected option remains available.

Save the exact source link and, where possible, the option code next to your notes. For clothing, compare the seller's measurement points with a similar item you already own. A flat chest width is not the same measurement as a body circumference. For shoes, check the size chart and measurement method, but do not infer comfort from a number alone. When the chart is missing or unclear, record the uncertainty instead of converting it into a promise about fit.

Keep a small evidence record

For each shortlisted item, write down the source link, date checked, chosen version, size, colour, quantity, displayed price and the detail that would matter most if it differed later. Save a copy of the relevant listing information for your own comparison where permitted. If a product has several similar options, retain the seller's original option code as well as any translation.

For example, a jacket record might say: “Black, option B, size L, one item; chest width shown in the chart; sleeve measurement unclear.” This is more useful than “black jacket, good quality.” It gives you a specific question to resolve and helps you compare the order with later information without relying on memory.

Keep personal details out of records shared publicly. Order numbers, addresses and complete tracking numbers belong in your private account or a support request, not in a public product comparison.

Understand the order and parcel as separate decisions

LoloBuy describes a workflow involving purchase assistance, warehouse inspection and international shipping. Details available for a particular item and the final conditions can change. Read the current order screen before paying, including the selected product, option, item amount and any domestic delivery or service charges shown.

The product price alone is not necessarily the cost of receiving the item. International freight is a later parcel decision. Depending on the order and destination, the budget may also need to cover packaging, optional services, taxes, duties or carrier handling. Some amounts cannot be known until the actual parcel is prepared. Keep those entries marked as unknown until a current quote is available.

To compare two shipping quotes fairly, use the same destination and the same parcel contents, weight, dimensions and service assumptions. Packaging can affect both actual and volumetric weight. A route shown to another buyer for a different parcel is not a guarantee that it will be available for yours. Check restrictions and the final charge in the current parcel flow.

Use QC photos for the questions they can answer

If photos of your received item are available, first check that the order reference and selected variant correspond to your notes. Compare broad details such as colour, quantity, visible labels, shape and included parts. Then inspect the features you identified in advance, such as a seam, fastener or measurement point. A seller's promotional image and a warehouse photo can serve different purposes, so identify the source of each image before comparing them.

Lighting, angle and image compression can make a small difference difficult to judge. A photo may support a visible observation, but it cannot establish authenticity, hidden damage, long-term durability, fabric composition or electrical safety. If a decisive detail is hidden, ask for the specific view or measurement where that option is available, and check any associated conditions or fee.

When a saved product link no longer works

A broken link does not prove that the seller has closed or that an alternative listing is identical. First check whether the address was copied in full and whether the page requires a sign-in or supports your region. If the service cannot read the link, consult the current order flow for the available next step. Do not invent a seller link, reuse an unrelated item number or pay for an option that you cannot identify.

If you find another listing, treat it as a new candidate. Compare the seller, option codes, measurements, photos, price and available quantity again. Similar titles and product images are not enough to establish that two listings refer to the same item. Update the date on your notes, keep the old record separate and revisit the budget before making a decision.

A practical shortlist before checkout

Before proceeding, ask five questions. Can you open the current product record? Is the chosen version identifiable? Is the sizing information sufficient for your decision? Have you separated the displayed item price from costs that will arise later? Do you know what evidence you would want to inspect if the item reaches the warehouse? If an answer is no, write down the missing information and decide whether clarification is available.

For two similar products, compare the same criteria side by side. The cheaper item may have an unclear size chart or a different package, while the other may still have an unknown international freight cost. A spreadsheet is most helpful when it makes those differences visible. It cannot tell you which compromise is right for you; it can prevent an unknown detail from disappearing behind a low displayed price.

Make the next decision while options are open

Separate what is confirmed from what is merely unclear. A visibly different size label calls for a different response from a colour that looks slightly warmer under unfamiliar lighting. Record the issue and the relevant evidence, then check the current order's support, return or exchange options and any applicable deadline. Eligibility and costs can depend on the seller, product and order stage; a request does not guarantee a refund.

Before submitting an international parcel, revisit unresolved questions, check the destination and available route conditions, and review the latest total. The purpose of a spreadsheet is to make product research more manageable. The useful result is a documented choice with its uncertainties visible, not an assumption that a link or photo has removed every risk.
