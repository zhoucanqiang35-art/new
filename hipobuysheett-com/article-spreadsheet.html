How to Use a Hipobuy Spreadsheet: From Product Finds to Your First Parcel

Learn how to compare product links, sizes, warehouse photos and parcel costs when using a Hipobuy spreadsheet.

What a spreadsheet can and cannot do

A Hipobuy spreadsheet is a product discovery list: it helps you find a listing and keep useful information together. A row is a starting point, not an inventory guarantee or proof of quality. The seller may change the listing, price, options or availability after a link is shared. This independent guide does not place orders or operate a warehouse.

Start with a category and a specific need. Record the item name, source link, colour, size, quantity and displayed price. If a row includes a note about materials or sizing, compare it with the current listing rather than assuming the note is still current.

Check the destination and the selected option

Open the matching product record and verify that it still describes the item you intended to research. Compare the picture and title with the available options. A link to a broad category is useful for browsing, but it is not evidence that a particular size or colour is available.

Translate option labels carefully and save the chosen variant before payment. A product may have several versions with different prices or measurements. If the description, photograph and option label disagree, seek clarification before placing an order; do not assume that the cheapest option is the complete item.

Compare measurements instead of size labels alone

Size letters are not a consistent measurement. Compare the seller’s chart with a garment that fits you, using the same measuring method. For shoes, check the listed foot or insole length and how it is measured. Leave room for differences in measurement and do not infer an exact fit from a single label.

Record the relevant dimensions alongside the selected variant. When warehouse photographs are available, compare the visible label and any actual measurements with your notes. A measurement cannot answer every question about comfort, but it can identify an obvious mismatch before shipment.

Separate item cost from the parcel budget

The displayed item price is only one part of a potential order. Before committing, check any domestic delivery, service, payment, international shipping, insurance and destination import charges that may apply. Fees and routes vary, and the current checkout or account information is the relevant source.

A useful worksheet has separate rows for item cost, local delivery, service charges, international transport and possible import costs. Keep estimates marked as estimates. Do not treat another buyer’s quote as a promise: destination, weight, dimensions, contents and route can change the result.

Review warehouse evidence before shipping

Compare warehouse photos with the exact order, not just a flattering seller image. Check item count, colour, size tag and obvious condition issues. Ask for a specific view or measurement when a decision depends on a detail that is not visible. An image cannot establish durability, authenticity or customs acceptance.

If something appears wrong, save the evidence and check the current seller and agent process promptly. Return eligibility and deadlines may differ by order. Once the items are ready, review packed weight, dimensions and route restrictions before submitting a parcel.

Keep a practical record

Keep the original listing, selected options, order details and relevant photographs together until delivery and any applicable after-sales period are complete. Remove links that no longer point to the intended listing and mark prices with the date observed.

To begin, browse the categories or product details on this site, then open the matching FindSpreadsheet record. Use the QC and parcel guides for the next decisions. This site is independent and does not guarantee a seller, price, delivery date or outcome.
