Product-link checks · updated 26 October 2026

Verify a PikoBuy product link before it becomes an order.

A spreadsheet row can help you discover an item, but it cannot freeze the source listing, the selected option or the eventual parcel. The useful work starts when you open the live link and create a record that can be compared with warehouse evidence later.

Scope: this independent guide does not verify sellers, place orders or promise that a listing is available. It explains how to make a source-link decision with less guesswork before you move from product research to checkout.

Start with the live listing, not its short title

Marketplace titles are often broad, translated unevenly or reused across several variations. Treat the title as a search clue, not a product specification. Open the source page and identify the seller or shop, the selected variation, size, colour, quantity and displayed price. Save the full URL and the time you checked it. If the page changes later, your record explains what you actually intended to buy.

PikoBuy’s public beginner guide describes submitting a product link or keywords, then choosing specifications before an order is submitted. That sequence matters: the right link with the wrong selected option is still the wrong order. A useful note records the exact option label instead of a vague phrase such as “black one” or “large.”

Build a short evidence record

  • Full source URL and shop name, saved before submitting
  • Selected size, colour, model, plug or configuration written exactly as shown
  • Quantity and displayed listing price, with currency context
  • Size chart, dimensions or compatibility notes saved with the listing
  • Listing photos that show the details you expect warehouse photos to confirm
  • Anything the page does not prove, marked as an uncertainty rather than a fact

This is not unnecessary paperwork. It lets you compare like with like when the item reaches a warehouse. It also prevents a common mistake: assuming that a product photo, a category name and a chosen variant all describe the same item.

Separate visible claims from assumptions

Some details are easy to see, while others are only implied. A photo can show colour, placement, obvious parts or an included accessory. A size chart can provide a measurement claim. Neither proves how an item will fit, how a material performs, whether a seller will keep stock, or whether a product meets a standard that is not shown on the page.

For clothing, keep the garment measurements and compare them with an item that fits you, rather than relying on a generic size letter. For shoes, save the stated size system and photos of the side, sole and heel. For bags and accessories, note dimensions, fastening and hardware. For electronics, record the exact model, plug or voltage requirement, included accessories and compatibility statement. If one of these details is missing, ask whether it is important enough to make the item unsuitable before you pay.

Use price as context, not as a promise

A source price is only one part of a possible purchase. PikoBuy’s public process describes an item-order stage and a separate parcel-shipping stage. A seller can change stock, option prices or domestic delivery terms; later, the parcel can have a different weight and size from the item you first noticed. Do not turn the source price into a delivered-cost claim.

Instead, compare the visible price with comparable listings and note what is included. A lower number can reflect a smaller option, a missing accessory, a different specification or a listing that needs more evidence. If a price looks unusual, the correct response is to inspect the option labels, photos and seller information—not to invent a quality conclusion.

Make the record usable for QC

When warehouse photos arrive, reopen your saved record before deciding what to do. Compare the selected variation, visible label, colour, dimensions, sole, clasp, screen, plug or included pieces against the exact evidence you saved. A warehouse image can clarify a visible mismatch; it cannot confirm every hidden defect or future performance claim. The goal is to identify an unresolved issue while a return window or additional-photo option may still be relevant.

Keep product research and parcel planning separate. A well-documented item can still be a poor parcel fit if it is bulky, fragile or restricted by a route. The next check should therefore be the QC-photo checklist, followed by the shipping-cost planning guide.

A repeatable five-minute review

  1. Open the live source link and select the intended option.
  2. Write down the option label, price, quantity and the details that matter for the category.
  3. Save the size chart, dimensions, compatibility information and relevant listing photos.
  4. Label anything uncertain; do not replace it with a reassuring assumption.
  5. Only then use the item as a shortlist candidate in the product database.

This approach is less exciting than chasing a fast-moving title, but it gives you a concrete comparison point at every later step. It is also the kind of information that makes a PikoBuy spreadsheet useful: clear discovery followed by a live, verifiable decision.
